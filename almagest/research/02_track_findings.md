## Small architectures with long-horizon memory for event sequences: smallest backbone that holds state over 10^4-10^5 events and produces relaxing (power-law decaying) responses

- [Certain] The original LOBS5 generator was about 6.3M parameters. It used 6 S5 layers, a 500-message context and 22 tokens per message, so the model sees an 11,000-token sequence. It also fed the L2 book as a separate continuous input branch, and the authors say adding book data cut training and validation loss sharply. — *6.3M params; 6 S5 layers; n=500 msgs; 22 tok/msg; 11,000 tokens; vocab 12,011* (arXiv 2309.00638 (Nagy et al. 2023), Sec 5.1, Fig 2)
- [Certain] LOB-Bench scaled LOBS5 to about 35M parameters by adding S5 layers. That was enough to drop the explicit error-correction step. Training took 30.4 L40 GPU-days. LOBS5 had the best impact-curve match in the benchmark (mean L1 dR = 2.45, against 126 for the 170M RWKV-6). So a small, structured SSM beat a model about 5x larger. — *6.3M to 35M; 30.4 L40-days; dR 2.45 vs 126 (RWKV-6 170M)* (arXiv 2502.09172 (LOB-Bench), App B and Sec 6)
- [Certain] An 8.2M Mamba-3 (d_model 256, 13 layers, 8 heads with a 64x128 state each, so 65,536 state values per layer) is competitive on LOB-Bench. Its context is 500 messages x 26 tokens = 13,000 tokens. Four of eight heads at the probe layer integrate order flow with half-lives of 28-66 messages; the other four forget within one message. In a random-weights twin, no head has a half-life above 10 messages. So long memory is learned through optimisation, not supplied by the architecture or its initialisation, and it reaches only about 10^2 messages. — *8.2M; 13 layers; d=256; 65,536 state/layer; half-life 28-66 msgs; random twin <=10 msgs; 58% of state write mass from newest message* (arXiv 2610.28753, Sec 4.1 and 5.1.3)
- [Certain] The 8.2M model's state mostly describes the present. The newest message accounts for 58% of total write magnitude and the last 50 messages for 77%. Reading out the decoded order-flow-imbalance (OFI) signal needs the last 56 messages to reach half its mass. A linear readout predicts the next-25-message mid move at only R^2 about 0.04, with no signal beyond about 100 messages. That is two to three orders of magnitude short of the 12k-73k-message metaorder horizon. — *58% newest msg; 77% last 50; half of OFI within 56 msgs; R^2 0.04 at H=25; no signal beyond H about 100* (arXiv 2610.28753, Fig 4 and Sec 5.2.2)
- [Certain] In LOB-Impact, the S5 family has the lowest exponent delta and the smallest free drift of all neural generators on every one of the 5 stocks. S5-120M delta is 0.63-0.98 and S5-4k is 0.69-1.05, against Mamba3/GDN at 0.79-1.21. S5-4k drift is between -4 and +18 ticks, against Mamba3-4k at down to -879. The 55M S5-4k is about as good as the 120M S5, so more size is not what helps. Going from 500 to 4k context did not fix relaxation: end/peak stays 1.11-1.24 for S5-4k. — *S5-120M delta: AMD .66, NVDA .98, GOOG .73, EA .84, MSFT .63; S5-4k (55M): .79/.97/.75/1.05/.69; Mamba3-4k: .93/1.11/1.21/1.12/.86, drift -879 GOOG* (arXiv 2610.27100, Table 3)
- [Likely] Inference from that table: S5's structured, HiPPO-initialised, weakly selective recurrence keeps the price balance better than input-selective Mamba-3 or GatedDeltaNet. Strong selectivity seems to let the models store persistence (trade-sign memory) without the matching mean reversion. This argues for a backbone where some of the memory is fixed and not gated by the input. (Inference from arXiv 2610.27100 Table 3 (Mamba H=0.58-0.62 vs S5 H=0.54-0.59))
- [Certain] The benchmark authors attribute the joint failure of the square-root exponent and relaxation to missing replenishment caused by limited long-horizon memory. Relaxation requires keeping a reference price from before the metaorder. A build-up rollout runs about 40k messages on AMD, against 500-message training windows. They suggest longer contexts and larger models as the next test. — *about 40k msgs per rollout; 500 or 4k context* (arXiv 2610.27100, Sec 2 and 4)
- [Certain] The empirical propagator is a slowly decaying power law tuned against trade-sign memory. Bouchaud et al. (2004) model the price as a sum of past signed trades weighted by a decaying kernel (the propagator). Trade-sign autocorrelation decays as l^-gamma (gamma about 0.2-0.24 for France Telecom), the kernel decays as l^-beta, and diffusive prices require beta_c = (1-gamma)/2. Their fit gives beta = 0.42 near beta_c = 0.38, with l0 = 20 trades. A plain exponential decay to a non-zero level gives unacceptable response shapes. They note that market makers using different horizons produce a sum of exponentials that easily mimics a power law. This is a physical motivation for a log-spaced mode bank. — *gamma about 0.24; beta fit 0.42; beta_c 0.38; l0 = 20 trades; impact peaks after 100-1000 trades* (arXiv cond-mat/0307332 (Bouchaud, Gefen, Potters, Wyart), Eqs 22-28 and Fig 9)
- [Certain] FRAC (NeurIPS 2026) builds a power-law memory into a selective SSM. It approximates the Caputo-fractional relaxation kernel E_alpha(-lambda t^alpha) with M = 16 fixed, log-spaced exponential modes (tau from 1 to 2^17), token-dependent alpha_t in (0,1) and lambda_t, and softmax read/write over modes with a -alpha log tau prior. Mean max kernel error is 6.8e-4 at M=16, against 1.34e-3 at M=8, and M=32 adds almost nothing. FRAC's recurrent state is 8x smaller than Mamba2/3 and 4.5x smaller than GatedDeltaNet at matched size. Freely learned timescales or removing the geometric grid both hurt. In a 7M-parameter DNA model (8 layers, d=256) it matches or beats Mamba3 and GatedDeltaNet up to 64K, with larger gains at long context. In a single-layer 200K-parameter model trained at length 512, it degrades least out to 128K on a heavy-tail probe. — *M=16; tau 1 to 2^17; error 6.82e-4 (M=16) vs 1.34e-3 (M=8); state 65,536 vs 524,288 scalars per layer; 7M DNA model to 64K; 200K-param probe to 128K; decode throughput near Mamba3* (arXiv 2609.36314, Sec 3.2, App E.3, E.4, G (Table 10), Fig 2 and 4)
- [Certain] Theory says power-law memory needs very few states. For a target kernel c j^-alpha, the best r-mode linear-state forecast error is e^-Theta(sqrt r), so reaching error tau needs only r = Theta(log^2(1/tau)) modes, built from positive log-spaced exponential quadrature. Finite-context error scales as L^(1-2alpha). For a contractive nonlinear recurrence, accuracy forces the contraction margin -log rho toward zero, so some modes must sit essentially at criticality (eigenvalues near 1). — *r* = Theta(log^2(1/tau)); context error Theta(L^(1-2alpha)); necessary -log rho <~ tau^(1/(2alpha-1)) log(e/tau)* (arXiv 2610.08816, Thm 1, Thm 12 and Corollary 7)
- [Certain] Standard initialisations cap memory far below 10^4-10^5 events. Griffin's RG-LRU initialises a^c uniformly in [0.9, 0.999], giving timescales of about 10-1000 steps. Its recurrence gate is biased toward keeping state (super-exponential memory by skipping uninformative inputs). Covering a 4x10^4-event metaorder needs eigenvalues down to about 1 - 2.5e-5 per event, or a slower clock. — *a in [0.9, 0.999] gives tau of about 10-1000 steps; c = 8* (arXiv 2402.19427 (Griffin), Sec 2.4; arithmetic tau = 1/(1-a))
- [Certain] A multi-scale SSM that gives each resolution its own state, plus a scale-dependent eigenvalue initialisation (slower scales closer to 1), reaches ListOps 63.04 against Mamba's 38.02. That beats Mamba with 2x parameters (49.63) or 2x state (42.14), with only O(KS) extra parameters per layer. Removing the multi-scale convolution drops it to 37.98. So multi-timescale structure beats raw size. — *63.04 vs 38.02 (Mamba), 49.63 (2x params), 42.14 (2x state)* (arXiv 2512.23824 (MS-SSM, COLM 2025), Tables 2 and 5)
- [Likely] Token-level encoding multiplies the memory horizon problem by 22-26x. A 40k-message metaorder is about 0.9-1.0M tokens under the LOBS5/Mamba-3 tokenisers. One recurrent step per event (factored event token: type, side, price, size, dt and reference in one embedding, with factored output heads) shrinks the required timescale range to 4x10^4 steps, which FRAC's tau_max = 2^17 already covers. — *40k msgs x 22-26 = 0.88-1.04M tokens vs 40k steps; 2^17 = 131,072* (Arithmetic from arXiv 2309.00638 (22 tok/msg) and 2610.28753 (26 tok/msg); tau range from 2609.36314)
- [Likely] S5 already supports irregular sampling via a per-step dt. In FRAC, the retention per step is exp(-dt * lambda^(1/alpha) / tau_m). Feeding inter-arrival time (or log dt) as dt makes decay run on physical time. The model then relaxes during quiet periods without needing generated messages, which is exactly what the LOB-Impact post-execution phase tests. (arXiv 2309.00638 Sec 3.2 (S5 varying time steps); arXiv 2609.36314 Eq 18-20)
- [Likely] Smaller is not the binding constraint. Every neural model from 55M to 120M fails relaxation on all 25 model-stock cells. The 8.2M model is competitive on LOB-Bench. The LOB-Bench KS ranking and the |delta-0.5| ranking are unstable or even negatively correlated (MSFT neural rows -0.90). Distributional fidelity bought with parameters does not buy correct impact, so a 5-20M model with the right memory law is a credible target. — *0/25 neural cells pass C5; KS vs |delta-0.5| rank corr +0.54 (GOOG), -0.71 (MSFT), -0.90 (MSFT neural)* (arXiv 2610.27100 Sec 3.4; arXiv 2610.28753 Sec 4.1)
- [Certain] Steering the 8.2M model's recurrent state moves its beliefs monotonically: buy probability goes 0.42 to 0.61 and P(exec) 0.002 to 0.090. But the effect carries into realised price only weakly: the mid stays inside the null band for order-flow pushes. Correct impact needs the generated replenishment (limit-order/cancel) flow to depend on long-memory state, not just a learned belief. — *buy prob 0.42 to 0.61; P(exec) 0.002 to 0.090; median exec volume 25 to 187 shares at alpha=+2.4* (arXiv 2610.28753, Sec 5.2.1)

**recommendations**

- Backbone: about 10M parameters (hard cap 20M). d_model 256, 8-10 residual blocks. Each block has a FRAC-style fractional mode-bank mixer: M=16 fixed log-spaced modes, tau from 1 to 2^17 events, learnable alpha_t and lambda_t, softmax read/write, plus a SwiGLU MLP. Basis: FRAC's 7M/8-layer/d=256 setting worked to 64K (2609.36314), and the 8.2M Mamba-3 at d=256 is LOB-Bench-competitive (2610.28753). Param split (Guessing): about 0.6-0.9M per block x 10, plus about 1-2M for embeddings and factored heads.
- Use one recurrent step per event, not 22-26 tokens per message. Sum the field embeddings into one event vector and use factored autoregressive output heads within the event (type, then side, price, size, dt, plus a pointer head for cancels as in ReLOBGen). This cuts the horizon by about 25x, so 10^4-10^5 events is within the mode-bank range.
- Run decay on physical time: set dt = f(inter-arrival time), with a learned monotone f, so modes relax during silence. Add a per-second slow clock with 1-2 extra mode-bank layers updated on wall-clock ticks. Together these form the planned two-clock memory and cover multi-minute metaorders at negligible cost.
- Fix the tail: freeze or regularise a subset of slow modes to be non-selective (input-independent retention, possibly a fixed alpha prior around 1-beta with beta about 0.4). This guarantees a positive power-law propagator, so the model cannot learn persistence without decay. Basis: S5 (less selective) beat Mamba3/GDN on delta and drift on all 5 stocks (2610.27100), and Bouchaud's beta_c = (1-gamma)/2.
- Do not initialise retention the Griffin/LRU way ([0.9, 0.999]). Spread eigenvalues geometrically down to 1 - 1e-5 per event, or use FRAC's grid. The random-weights twin of the 8.2M model had no head above a 10-message half-life, so long memory must be built in, not hoped for.
- Keep a short local window: a depthwise causal conv of 4, or a small 64-128-event sliding attention in 2 of the blocks, for queue mechanics and pointer/cancel reference. Leave long-range causation to the mode bank (the Griffin hybrid pattern, 2402.19427).
- Cheap ablation ladder before scaling: (a) Mamba-3 8M at 1 step/event; (b) the same with FRAC mixer; (c) (b) plus physical-time dt plus frozen slow modes; (d) (c) plus slow clock. Score each with the LOB-Impact phases (delta, Y, end/peak, H, drift). Only scale parameters if (d) still fails C4/C5.
- Add an auxiliary training target: regress realised mid-price against the model's internal propagator readout, sum_k G(l) eps_k with a power-law G. That gives the slow modes a direct gradient toward relaxation even before impact-law post-training.

**risks**

- FRAC has not been tested on LOB data or on generation. Its gains are long-context recall and perplexity, not counterfactual response.
- The diagnosis that relaxation fails for lack of memory comes from the LOB-Impact authors and is stated as tentative. The real cause could be the training objective or data (no visible metaorders), and then any backbone would fail without post-training.
- Collapsing messages into one step per event loses within-message token conditioning. Factored heads must keep the cancel/pointer references valid (Guessing on quality cost).
- Modes with eigenvalues near 1 risk optimisation instability and accumulated drift over very long rollouts. 2610.08816 shows near-criticality is necessary but not sufficient.
- The param-budget breakdown is an estimate, not measured.

## Hybrid physics + neural LOB generators with square-root impact and relaxation built in, so a small network (20M parameters or fewer) is enough

- [Certain] In an agent-based LOB that puts each mechanism to an ablation test, only two components are jointly necessary for the square-root law: order splitting, and active replenishment by market makers. Removing splitting drops delta from 0.549 to 0.324, and removing HFT replenishment drops it to 0.386. Momentum traders, price limits, the splitting rule (Dirichlet, uniform or front-loaded) and background liquidity each move delta by less than 10%. The shape of the visible book does not set delta: the LOB-walking formula 1/(1+gamma) under-predicts it. The authors read the result as support for the latent-liquidity picture. — *Baseline delta 0.549+/-0.013; no splitting 0.324+/-0.015; no HFT 0.386+/-0.008; 2000 stocks <delta>=0.539+/-0.048; TSE benchmark 0.489; HFT replenish probability p=0.9 after each fill* (arXiv 2607.04280 (Zhou, Chen, Wei), Table 1 and Sec. 5)
- [Likely] Implication for Almagest: in an impact test the injected metaorder supplies the splitting, so the generator must supply the replenishment. It has to refill depleted levels during the gaps between child orders, on timescales that span the metaorder duration (12k-73k messages). The LOB-Impact models fail because their memory (500-4k context) is shorter than that refill-and-recall timescale. They therefore behave like the 'no HFT' or 'no memory' ablation, which matches the measured delta 0.63-1.21 and the missing relaxation. (Inference combining 2607.04280 ablations with the 2610.27100 numbers given in the task context)
- [Certain] The LLOB (Donier-Bonart-Mastromatteo-Bouchaud) reduces bid/ask reaction-diffusion to a LINEAR equation for the imbalance phi = rho_B - rho_A: d_t phi = D d_xx phi - nu phi + lambda sign(p - x) + m_t delta(x - y_t). The reaction rate kappa drops out exactly, and the price is the zero of phi. With a constant-rate metaorder the exact solution is y_t = A sqrt(Dt), which gives I ~ sqrt(Q/L) with L = J/D. Free decay after the metaorder starts as 1 - sqrt((t-T)/T) for every value of m0/J, then falls as sqrt(T/t). The model is proven free of price manipulation. — *A ~ m0/(J sqrt(pi)) for m0<<J; A ~ sqrt(2 m0/J) for m0>>J; latent memory 1/nu of hours to days; linear region ~1% of price* (arXiv 1412.0141 (Donier et al. 2015), Eqs. 2, 9, 15, 17, 50)
- [Certain] The single-timescale LLOB gives a participation-independent Y only when m0 >> J. At realistic participation (m0 < 0.1J) it reduces to a linear propagator with the sqrt(phi) 'wart' (Y proportional to sqrt(phi)). For any linear self-similar kernel G ~ tau^-beta, I ~ phi^beta Q^(1-beta), so getting sqrt(Q) forces the sqrt(phi) wart. A fixed linear kernel therefore cannot pass both the shape test and the amplitude test. (arXiv 2610.10053 (Bouchaud, Mastromatteo, Toth 2026), Sec. 1 Eq. 3; arXiv 1710.03734 Sec. 4.1)
- [Certain] Fix: a multi-timescale LLOB with fast agents (HFT: nu_f T >> 1) and slow agents (nu_s T -> 0). Impact crosses over from linear (t << t*) to square root with Y independent of m0 (t >> t*), where x_t = (lambda_f/(L_s nu_f))(sqrt(1 + t/t*) - 1). This holds for J_s << m0 << J. A finite slowest memory 1/nu_s yields permanent impact that is linear in Q, independent of trading rate and consistent with no-arbitrage. A power-law spectrum of memory times resolves the diffusivity puzzle (persistent order flow combined with diffusive price). — *With nu_f = 1/s and J_s = 0.1J, the square-root regime holds for participation > 3e-4; permanent I_inf = (1/2) xi_c (Q/Q_lin); with a power-law spectrum and gamma=1/2, impact ~ Q^(5/8)* (arXiv 1710.03734 (Benzaquen & Bouchaud), Eqs. 18-21, Sec. 3, Sec. 5)
- [Certain] Empirical relaxation target: on ANcerno (about 8M metaorders), impact at the same-day close is 0.66+/-0.04 of peak, matching the 2/3 predicted by the FGLW fair-pricing argument (ratio 1/beta with Pareto tail beta = 1.5). Decay continues afterwards: next-day close is about 0.83 of same-day close, and the long-run plateau is about 1/2 of the first-day impact (about 1/3 of peak). Intraday decay fits the propagator form (1+z)^(1-beta) - z^(1-beta) with beta = 0.22, with z measured in volume time. Moro et al. report permanent/peak ratios of 0.51 (Spanish market) and 0.69 (London). — *I_SC/I_SE = 0.66+/-0.04; I_SC2 = 0.83 I_SC; I_inf ~ 0.42 (0.35-0.61 across market caps); propagator beta = 0.22* (arXiv 1901.05332 (Bucci et al.), Fig. 1-3; arXiv 1102.5457 (FGLW), Eq. 21 and Sec. VII.B)
- [Likely] Mechanism-to-target map. delta ~ 0.5 comes from diffusive (Abel 1/sqrt(t)) memory of the consumed latent liquidity, a locally linear book and splitting. Amplitude Y = O(1), independent of phi, needs a slow liquidity pool separate from the fast J. Relaxation toward about 2/3 of peak needs a kernel that decays after T with the 1 - sqrt((t-T)/T) shape, plus a finite slowest memory nu_s or an informational drift term (Gamma Q) setting the plateau. All of these are explicit low-dimensional terms (D, nu_k, lambda_k, L_k, Gamma). None needs to be learned by a large network. (Synthesis of arXiv 1412.0141 Secs. V-IX, 1710.03734, 1901.05332)
- [Certain] Clock separation: sign long-memory (Lillo-Mike-Farmer) is an event-time statement, while square-root impact is an operational-time statement about the front of a locally linear latent book under damped diffusion. The square-root law y(u) - y0 ~ (m0/L_u) sqrt(u/(pi D_u)) survives intact, and heavy-tailed calendar waiting times change only the projection (impact ~ t^(beta/2)). So the physics module should run on operational or event time, not wall-clock time. (arXiv 2609.13715 (Angstmann & Gebbie), Eqs. 7, 11-12, 19)
- [Certain] A linear propagator that is blind to who is trading prices an isolated metaorder at phi^((1-gamma)/2) Q^((1+gamma)/2). At gamma ~ 1/2 that is Q^(3/4): under-concave, with a residual phi^(1/4). A clean square-root law from a propagator needs either an age clock (a two-time kernel that depends on time since the metaorder started, as in Maitrier-Bouchaud) or the LLOB volume-balance mechanism. Separately, a volume-conservation argument singles out delta = 1/2 as the only exponent for which the daily variance budget closes, independent of the number of simultaneous metaorders. The authors point to the latent-book picture as the natural home of the square-root law. — *Isolated metaorder impact ~ phi^(1/4) Q^(3/4) at gamma=1/2; empirical Y/b ~ 1/3 giving Y ~ 0.32 (end-of-day-decayed)* (arXiv 2610.10053, Eq. 10, Secs. 4.3, 6, 8)
- [Guessing] Implication: Almagest's measured delta of 0.63-1.21 (above 0.5, near 0.75 in the low cases) and amplitudes of 1.7-50 are consistent with networks acting as participant-blind linear or under-damped propagators. They are not the Q^(3/4) of 2610.10053 exactly, but sit on the same side. Adding parameters will not fix this; the structure has to change, through a latent-liquidity state or an age clock. (Inference from 2610.10053 Eq. 10 vs. 2610.27100 numbers in task context)
- [Likely] Gatheral's no-dynamic-arbitrage constraint: exponential impact decay (Obizhaeva-Wang resilience) is compatible only with LINEAR impact. With power-law impact f(v) ~ v^delta and kernel G ~ t^-gamma, ruling out price manipulation requires gamma + delta >= 1. The square-root law with a 1/sqrt(t) kernel (delta = gamma = 1/2) sits exactly on that boundary. An Obizhaeva-Wang-style exponential resilience module therefore cannot be paired with square-root impact. Use power-law or multi-exponential kernels, or the LLOB, which is proven manipulation-free. (Gatheral 2010, Quantitative Finance 10(7):749 (abstract, via https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:taf:quantf:v:10:y:2010:i:7:p:749-759); gamma+delta>=1 as restated in arXiv 1412.4839; LLOB no-manipulation proof in arXiv 1412.0141)
- [Certain] Itkin's GLE latent-liquidity model writes memory kernels as finite sums of exponentials, so it has an exact Markovian lift (a few auxiliary states). A threshold-activated counterflow gives an intermediate square-root regime between linear limits, I_T(Q) = sqrt(Q/(omega T)) tanh(sqrt(omega Q T)/L0), with nonnegative round-trip cost. Two intrinsic and two order-flow modes are enough. The square-root range requires T >> tau_cf, and pool depletion narrows it, pushing the local exponent to about 0.59-0.61 at Q0. A sum of exponentials maps directly onto SSM-style diagonal state, which makes it a cheap, differentiable physics module. — *Fresh-pool local exponent 0.51 at Q0, T=tau0; single-mode 0.59, GLE 0.61; ratio of square-root bounds (T/tau_cf)^2* (arXiv 2609.37872 (Itkin 2026), Prop. 4 Eq. 45, Eq. 46-47, Sec. 7.1, Table 1)
- [Certain] Neural QR precedent showing small networks suffice once the structure is right: the Deep/Multidimensional Queue-Reactive model is a Poisson point process whose intensities come from an MLP. For DQR it is [128,32] tanh; for MDQR, intensity [256,64] with 30 outputs and order size [256,64] with 200 outputs, about 0.1M parameters in total by layer count. On Bund futures it reproduces the square-root law, but the concave-then-relax impact profile appeared only after trade-imbalance features (an explicit slow order-flow memory) were added; without them impact was linear then plateaued. — *MDQR input dim 25/27, hidden [256,64]; parameter count is my inference from the layer sizes (~0.1M)* (arXiv 2501.08822 (Bodor & Carlier), Tables 2 and 4, Fig. 7)
- [Certain] A minimal non-Markovian mechanism can reproduce concave impact and reversion. The NMZI model makes the sign probability of limit orders a sigmoid of an exponentially weighted mid-price return (memory 1/beta, strength alpha), so replenishment reacts to price. This gives a concave path during execution and post-execution reversion with 2 parameters. Decay is exponential, however, and the concavity transitions to a linear stationary regime for long metaorders, so it lacks true square-root asymptotics. (arXiv 2503.05254 (Ravagnani & Lillo), Secs. 4.1, 4.5-4.6, Table 1)

**recommendations**

- Concrete hybrid design, 'Almagest-LL', about 8-15M parameters in total. (1) PHYSICS CORE, with fewer than 100 learnable scalars: a discretized multi-timescale LLOB imbalance field phi_k(y) for K = 3-4 liquidity classes (fast or HFT, medium, slow, ultra-slow) on a 128-256-bin price grid relative to the latent price. Each class has (D, nu_k, lambda_k, L_k), plus an informational drift Gamma. Step it in operational or event time (2609.13715) with an explicit Abel/heat-kernel update, or with the exact sum-of-exponentials lift of 2609.37872, written as a diagonal SSM in JAX. Injected orders enter as the source m_t delta(y - y_t), and the latent price is the zero of the summed phi. Per 1412.0141 and 1710.03734 this gives the square-root law with Y independent of m0 when J_s << m0 << J, the 1 - sqrt((t-T)/T) relaxation, and a permanent plateau linear in Q set by nu_slowest. (2) NEURAL EVENT HEAD, 5-15M: a small per-event SSM (S5/Mamba, 4-6 layers, d = 256-384) that generates factored event tokens for the VISIBLE book. It is conditioned on features read from the physics core: local liquidity slopes L_k, phi at offsets of +/-1..16 ticks, cumulative injected flow, and the age since the latest same-sign flow burst (an age clock, per 2610.10053). (3) COUPLING: the visible mid is soft-tethered to the latent price through a learned bounded restoring intensity. The network models the residual microstructure (spread, queue sizes, cancels, timing); the physics owns the slow drift. That removes free drift (-879 ticks) and the excess Hurst exponent by construction, because long-horizon price motion is set by phi and V_t.
- Make the two-clock memory the LLOB itself: the per-second slow memory slot is the K-class latent field, which lives in operational time, and the per-event SSM handles fine order flow. This is cheaper than stretching the neural context to 12k-73k messages, which is what the 78-120M LOB-Impact models would need and do not have.
- Calibrate physics parameters before neural training, using closed-form targets: L_s from Y in [0.5,1] via I = Y sigma sqrt(Q/V); nu_f and J_s/J from the linear-to-square-root crossover participation (3e-4 per 1710.03734); nu_slowest from the end-of-day ratio of about 0.66 and the long-run ratio of about 0.42 (1901.05332); and the D/sigma relation from the daily volatility. Fit on LOBSTER and crypto L3 metaorder proxies (consecutive same-account or same-direction runs). This replaces most of stage-3 impact-law post-training with a cheap one-off fit.
- Keep the neural part from overriding impact: (a) give the residual intensity head bounded output (tanh-scaled) and penalize its integrated response to injected flow beyond the physics prediction; (b) add a manipulation-free regularizer, under which round-trip injected orders must have nonnegative expected cost (checkable in-batch through counterfactual shared-noise rollouts, which reuses the Causal PFN shared-noise trick, 2610.12537); (c) never use single-exponential resilience paired with square-root impact (Gatheral: requires gamma + delta >= 1).
- In the synthetic stage-1 prior, ablate replenishment and memory explicitly following 2607.04280: generate worlds with and without splitting and replenishment, and with nu spectra spanning 3 decades, so the neural head learns to read liquidity state from the physics features rather than memorizing one regime. This matches 2610.20104, which finds regime diversity, not model size, limits generalization.
- Cheapest baseline to beat first, roughly 0.1-1M parameters: a multi-timescale LLOB core plus an MDQR-style MLP intensity head (2501.08822), with trade-imbalance and latent-slope features. Run it through the LOB-Impact tests (delta, Y, end/peak, visible/blind, Hurst, drift) before scaling the event SSM. If it passes delta and relaxation, every additional parameter is for microstructure realism, not impact.
- Add to the eval suite the falsifiable tests from 2610.10053 and 1412.0141: (i) a buy-then-sell reversal, where LLOB predicts return to the start price at about T/4 in the linear regime and faster in the nonlinear one; (ii) an isolated metaorder separated from earlier ones versus one inside a stream; (iii) phi-independence of Y at fixed Q across participation 0.1%-10%.

**risks**

- The LLOB square-root law at realistic low participation depends on a fast/slow liquidity split that cannot be measured directly. The latent field is unobservable, so L_s, nu_s and J_s are identified only through impact data, which creates a circularity risk in calibration (1412.0141, 1710.03734, 2610.10053 Sec. 1).
- With a power-law memory spectrum, 1710.03734 predicts Q^(5/8) (about 0.6) rather than 0.5. The pass band |delta - 0.5| <= 0.10 tolerates it, but only just.
- The LLOB's mean-reverting confinement can make prices sub-diffusive (H too LOW) unless an informational drift V_t correlated with flow (Gamma) is added. Tuning Gamma trades permanent impact against diffusivity (1412.0141 Sec. IX). The excess-Hurst problem could flip sign.
- 2610.10053 argues that a clean square-root law for an ISOLATED metaorder needs an onset or age clock that anonymous markets may not possess. An age feature built into the generator could leak information that a real market does not have, inflating apparent realism in what-if and RL use.
- The coupling between the neural visible book and the physics latent price may be gamed during training: the network could learn to ignore the tether or fight it. This needs a bounded parameterization and explicit counterfactual (visible versus blind) checks.
- The empirical relaxation level depends on horizon and on metaorder autocorrelation (0.66 at same-day close, about 0.42 long-run after deconvolution, per 1901.05332). The benchmark's end/peak target must specify its horizon in volume time, or calibration will chase the wrong number.
- Gatheral's gamma + delta >= 1 condition is restated from secondary sources (1412.4839 and search snippets); I did not read the original paper directly.

**open_questions**

- What end/peak horizon (in volume time or messages after T) does LOB-Impact 2610.27100 use for its relaxation pass criterion, and is it the same-day ~2/3 or the long-run ~1/2-1/3 target?
- Can the K-class latent field be identified from L3 crypto data, where account-level or self-trade patterns may reveal real metaorders, better than from anonymous LOBSTER data?
- Does Itkin's GLE with a finite-exponential lift (2609.37872) or the discretized LLOB PDE give better delta and relaxation per FLOP inside a JAX SSM? The companion calibration paper is not out yet.
- Maitrier & Bouchaud's two-time (age-dependent) propagator is cited in 2610.10053 but I did not read it. Would it work as a lighter alternative to the full LLOB grid?
- Does the 8.2M Mamba-3 interpretability result (imbalance heads with half-lives of 28-66 messages, 2610.28753) mean the existing models only have the fast class and lack the slow class? If so, adding the physics slow pool directly addresses the failure.

## Stage-1 synthetic generators for causal pretraining: known agent types, planted metaorders and spoofers

- [Certain] In a synthetic study, model size and token count stop helping early, and the number of distinct regimes is what limits generalization. A model trained on 1 regime fails on new regimes. Generalization reaches the unlimited-diversity limit only near K=256 regimes. The entropy floor is reached by models of about 1e5 parameters. Models ranged from 25k to 31.5M parameters; the main model was 14.2M. The whole study took about 100 runs and 55 GPU-hours on a single-GPU workstation. — *K=256; 1-regime loss 3.57 vs 1.34 floor; capacity binds below ~1e5 params; S2=14.2M; 131k tok/s training; 55 GPU-h* (arXiv 2610.20104 (Acero, J.P. Morgan), Sec 1, 3.3, Fig 5-6, Table 1)
- [Certain] The 2610.20104 generator ladder is a cheap template for Stage 1. It has one price-time-priority engine and four families: F1 Poisson, F2 Hawkes (branching a~U(0.1,0.85), decay beta LogU(0.5,20)), F3 four-agent ABM (noise, momentum, mean-reverter, market maker; mix ~Dir(1,1,1,1)) and F4 nonstationary heavy-tailed. Each episode is 2,000 burn-in messages plus 16,384 recorded. Adding F4 cut zero-shot size loss on real NASDAQ data from 1.60 to 0.91 nats, closing 98% of the gap. Limits: agent identities are hidden, cancels remove a random resting order (no order IDs), and there are no injected or exogenous orders and no impact measurement (listed as future work). — *vocab 53 tokens, 4 tokens/message, 512-message windows; size loss 1.60->0.91 nats* (arXiv 2610.20104, Sec 3.1-3.2, Table 1, Table 6, Limitations (v))
- [Certain] A minimal 4-agent LOB ABM reproduces the square-root law. Agents: institutional splitters (Pareto Q, xi~U[1.5,3.5], 5-50 child orders, Poisson gaps), HFT market makers that replenish with p=0.9 after a fill, retail noise and news shocks. Mean delta over 2000 stocks is 0.539±0.048 against a TSE benchmark of 0.489. Ablations show both splitting and replenishment are necessary: without splitting delta falls to 0.324, without HFT replenishment to 0.386. Momentum, price limits and splitting rule move delta by less than 10%. 2000 stocks x 1e6 steps took about 3 h on 40 cores. Code is available only on request, and relaxation was not measured. — *delta 0.539±0.048 (range 0.449-0.633); c 0.934±0.130; no-split 0.324; no-HFT 0.386* (arXiv 2607.04280 (Zhou, Chen, Wei), Abstract, Sec 2, Table 1)
- [Certain] The plain queue-reactive (QR) model has no relaxation: after a metaorder ends the book evolves as if nothing happened, and impact grows linearly with volume. Adding a signed-flow state phi_t = sum G(t-t_k)·eps_k·sqrt(V_k) with kernel G(t)=(1+t/tau)^-3/2, tau=50s, and a one-sided trade-probability tilt exp(m[phi]+) with m=0.036 gives concave impact during execution and partial reversion afterwards. This was calibrated on 2y of Databento MBP-10 data for PFE, INTC, VZ and T, and checked over 500k simulated metaorders. Symmetrising bid and ask removes artificial drift. The kernel can be approximated by a sum of exponentials for O(1) updates per event. The paper says code is on GitHub; the license was not checked. — *tau=50s, m=0.036 (0.035 in appendix), G~t^-3/2, 500k metaorders* (arXiv 2603.24137 (Noble, Rosenbaum, Souilmi), Sec 2.7, 2.9, 4.1, Fig 10, Fig 15, App D-E)
- [Certain] JAX-LOB stores every resting order as [Price, Qty, OrderID, TraderID, Ts, Tns] in fixed arrays, and every trade logs both the aggressive and the standing order ID. Agent labels and order-level attribution therefore come built in. Run with vmap over 1000 books on a 2080Ti, the effective cost is about 2.6 µs per message, against 3.6-37.9 µs on a CPU. Env step time is 0.46 ms at 10k parallel environments. Under vmap the cost depends on book capacity, not message type (capacity 1000 costs about 11.5 ms per vmapped message). No LICENSE file was visible on the GitHub page. — *2.6 µs/msg effective @1000 books; 0.46 ms/step @10k envs; capacity 10/100/1000 -> 1.4/2.6/11.75 ms* (arXiv 2308.13289 Sec 4, Tables 4-6; https://github.com/KangOxford/jax-lob)
- [Certain] JaxMARL-HFT (Apache-2.0) extends JAX-LOB to heterogeneous multi-agent environments (market maker, execution, directional). The paper names labelling the origin of orders as a supported use. On one GPU with 4000 envs it runs 351,119 steps/s at 1 message per step and 21,969 steps/s at 100 messages per step. The same benchmark gives PyMarketSim 10,830/463 and ABIDES-gym 2,979/734 steps/s. The background order flow is LOBSTER replay, not reactive, and impact measurement is listed as future work. — *351k vs 10.8k (PyMarketSim) vs 3.0k (ABIDES-gym) steps/s at 1 msg/step; up to 240x faster MARL training* (arXiv 2511.02136 Table 1, Sec 4, Sec 6; https://github.com/vmohl/JaxMARL-HFT (README: Apache License 2.0))
- [Likely] ABIDES and ABIDES-gym (Python, discrete-event, NASDAQ ITCH/OUCH-like messages) have agent IDs and documented impact studies. Byrd et al. found impact concave and decreasing in the market-order fraction. No sqrt exponent or relaxation was reported in the reviewed sources. Memory limits it: Python ran out of memory at about 450 agents on 16 GB, while MAXE fits 100k agents in about 100 MiB. The license appears to be BSD-3 according to a fork's README, and the JPMC repo is listed as a public archive. — *ABIDES OOM ~450 agents/16GB; MAXE 100,022 agents <100MiB* (arXiv 2402.17359 Sec 7 (impact review); arXiv 2008.07871 Sec 6; https://github.com/purohitank/abides-jpmc-public; https://github.com/jpmorganchase/)
- [Certain] ABIDES-MARL (ETH) adds synchronized multi-learner execution on top of ABIDES-gym. Its execution experiments use a reduced-form Kyle-style pro-rata VWAP book, not price-time priority, so it is not a source of order-level impact data. Code: github.com/10258392511/MARLOptExecution. (arXiv 2511.02016 Sec 2, Def 3.2, footnote 1)
- [Likely] PyMarketSim (Python, MIT per the ICAIF'24 paper) is the Wellman-group simulator with ZI and heuristic-belief-learning agents plus a private-valuation model. It is the closest open lineage to planted spoofing. Wang and Wellman (AAMAS 2017) and Wang et al. (Games 2021) built spoofing agents in this ABM family and found spoofing hurts surplus. A 2024 follow-up found spoofing much less profitable in high liquidity. This makes it a ready reference for spoofer behaviour, but at about 10.8k steps/s on one CPU core it is too slow for bulk pretraining data. — *10,830 steps/s (1 msg/step, 1 core)* (https://anrigu.github.io/files/camera_ready_MarketSim.pdf; https://ifmas.csc.liv.ac.uk/Proceedings/aamas2017/pdfs/p651.pdf; https://mdpi.com/2073-4336/12/2/46; https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3188796/1/mainICAIF24_CR_nonacm.pdf; arXiv 2511.02136 Table 1)
- [Certain] MAXE (C++ core, Python API, multithreaded) supports pro-rata and FIFO matching, latency and impact agents. After a large market order (10-100x the average size) it shows climb, fall, overreaction and settlement phases. Long-term impact is reported as roughly linear in delay, with log impact growing with volume. No sqrt-law measurement. Repo: github.com/maxe-team/maxe; license not checked. (arXiv 2008.07871 Sec 3-4, Fig 4-5)
- [Certain] Hawkes-based LOB simulators have built-in reactivity. Bacry-Iuga et al. (2015) report concave impact during a metaorder and convex relaxation after it. Plain Poisson and plain Hawkes LOBs are reported to show no clear impact trend under TWAP/POV execution. A 2025 C++ engine with Hawkes order flow calibrated a branching ratio of about 0.8 on BTCUSDT, but it has no dependence on book state. tick (BSD-3) provides fast Hawkes simulation and fitting. — *BTCUSDT branching n=0.80-0.81; AAPL n=0.82* (arXiv 2402.17359 Sec 7; arXiv 2510.08085 Tables 3-4, Sec 8.2; https://raw.githubusercontent.com/X-DataInitiative/tick/master/README.md)
- [Likely] Latent-liquidity / LLOB theory and ABMs explain where the sqrt law comes from: impact is softened by hidden orders that are revealed as price diffuses. This gives delta=1/2 independently of the visible book shape. The ABMs are Mastromatteo, Toth and Bouchaud (PRE 2014, with stimulated refill) and Donier et al. (QF 2015). It matches the 2607.04280 ablation that splitting plus replenishment are required. It also matches LOB-Impact's diagnosis that missing replenishment explains why neural generators fail the impact tests. (arXiv 2607.04280 Sec 1, 5.3 (citing PRE 89 042805, QF 15 1109); arXiv 2610.27100)
- [Likely] mbt_gym (Python, Jerome et al., ICAIF 2023) offers vectorised model-based environments (Avellaneda-Stoikov-type midprice and fill models) for market making and execution. It is not an order-level LOB, so it has no order IDs and no emergent impact. It is only useful as a regime-diversity source for price and fill dynamics. License not verified. (https://github.com/jjjerome/mbt_gym; arXiv 2209.07823)

**recommendations**

- Build a single JAX generator ('almagest-sim') instead of adopting ABIDES or PyMarketSim. Use JAX-LOB-style fixed-array books with OrderID and TraderID per order (2308.13289), vmapped over 1k-10k books. Expect about 1e5-1e6 messages/s per GPU, extrapolating from the 2.6 µs/message effective figure and JaxMARL-HFT's 351k steps/s (Likely, not measured end-to-end). Emit order ID, agent ID, agent type and a metaorder/spoof flag on every message so Stage 1 gets exact labels for free.
- Agent mix per episode (sample everything from priors so that K≥256 distinct regimes, 2610.20104): (a) the F1/F2/F4 Poisson, Hawkes and nonstationary flows from 2610.20104 as background; (b) the 2607.04280 ecology of institutional splitters (Pareto Q, 5-50 child orders) plus HFT market makers that replenish with p~U(0.6,0.95), which gives delta≈0.5; (c) the QR state-dependent intensities plus the power-law flow-memory tilt G=(1+t/tau)^-3/2 with tau≈50s (2603.24137), which gives relaxation and partial reversion; (d) spoofer and layering agents that post far-side size away from the touch, cancel on an adverse move and trade on the near side, after the Wang and Wellman spec. Randomise tau, m, replenishment rate, branching ratio and agent weights per regime.
- Use the 'turn off a mechanism' ablations as causal counterfactual pairs. For each seed, generate the same episode with and without the planted metaorder (shared noise, as in Causal PFN 2610.12537), and with replenishment on and off. This teaches the model what impact the planted order itself caused, and gives free pass/fail checks: delta must fall to about 0.32-0.39 when splitting or replenishment is removed.
- Before any training, validate the generator against the LOB-Impact tests: delta within 0.5±0.1, Y in [0.5,1], end/peak ratio below 1 (relaxation), Hurst exponent near 0.5 and no free drift (symmetrise bid/ask as in 2603.24137). Only keep regimes whose parameters pass, plus a labelled 'broken-physics' minority for contrast.
- Episode length must cover full metaorder plus relaxation windows, because LOB-Impact metaorders span 12k-73k messages. Use episodes of at least 100k messages for the impact families, even though 2610.20104 used 16,384.
- Model size: 2610.20104 hit the synthetic floor with about 1e5 parameters and a single GPU, so a 5-20M Stage-1 model is more than enough capacity. Spend compute on regime count and long-memory architecture, not parameters. Budget: about 1-2B tokens of synthetic data per family is in line with the 1.6B tokens per family used there.
- Keep ABIDES, PyMarketSim and MAXE only as external sanity-check references for spoofer behaviour and impact shape, with a few hundred episodes each. At roughly 3k-11k steps/s on CPU (2511.02136), they are 30-100x too slow for bulk pretraining data.

**risks**

- The JAX-LOB license is unclear because no LICENSE file was visible. A reimplementation, or forking from JaxMARL-HFT (Apache-2.0), avoids the legal risk.
- The sqrt law in 2607.04280 comes from a minimal ABM whose code is available on request only. The calibrated constants are model-internal and its visible book is too concave (gamma 1.5-3.6 against an empirical value of about 1).
- The QR impact kernel in 2603.24137 is fitted to a theoretical target (Jusselin-Rosenbaum) on large-tick US equities. Applying it to crypto L3 or small-tick assets needs recalibration.
- Synthetic-to-real transfer depends on how well the prior covers real flow (2610.20104). Spoofer agents written from academic specs may not resemble real layering, so detection trained on synthetic data could transfer poorly.
- Under vmap, JAX array books cost scales with book capacity (11.75 ms per vmapped message at capacity 1000). A deep book combined with layering agents could cut throughput sharply.
- None of the surveyed open simulators publishes a joint delta + Y + relaxation + Hurst validation. Our mix would be the first, so it has to be verified rather than assumed.

**open_questions**

- What throughput does a JAX engine actually reach end-to-end with Hawkes thinning plus agent policies and spoofer logic? There is no published number for this combination.
- Does the QR power-law tilt combined with HFT replenishment give delta≈0.5 and Y in [0.5,1] at the same time, or do they interact and push the amplitude Y outside that range?
- What licenses do the Noble et al. QR code and mbt_gym use?
- Does pretraining on regimes with planted relaxation actually fix the 'no relaxation' failure in 4k-context models, or is long memory (the two-clock design) still required?
- How many of the K≥256 regimes have to contain metaorders or spoofers before impact-aware representations appear? 2610.20104 tested regime diversity only for Hawkes parameters.

## Evaluation + leakage-proof harness (impact gates, splits, statistics, checkpoint-selection hygiene, manipulation-detection eval) for a 5-20M-param order-level LOB generator

- [Certain] The LOB-Impact claim ladder has fixed criteria that apply to every model. C1 (flow balance): gamma_flow, Hurst H and free drift must each sit within a tolerance of the stock's own replay. C2 (existence): the identifiability gate finds a law. C3 (attribution): the visible peak is at least 3x the blind floor. C4 (square-root law): |delta-0.5|<=0.10 and Y in [0.5,1]. C5 (relaxation): end-over-peak is at or below the Obizhaeva-Wang kernel's value on the same stock and schedule, the post-execution slope is non-positive, and free drift is under 20 ticks. C3-C5 count as 'undecidable', not as a failure, when the measurement behind them is missing. — *C1 tolerances: gamma_flow 0.10, H 0.02, drift 5 ticks. C3 v/b>=3. C4 |delta-0.5|<=0.10, Y in [0.5,1]. C5 drift<20 ticks, slope<=0. All 25 neural cells: C1 fail, C2 and C3 pass, C4 and C5 fail.* (arXiv 2610.27100 Sec 2.1 (p.4))
- [Certain] The injection protocol is calibrated per stock from held-out data and never per model. The participation target is eta=10%, the child size is the median market-order size, and spacing is DeltaN=(1/eta-1)/phi=9/phi, where phi is the market-order share of messages. A build-up metaorder is N=100 children (i=100, c=0). The relaxation shape is i=10 children followed by c=100 silent blocks. Buy and sell runs are combined antisymmetrically, I=(I_buy-I_sell)/2, which cancels direction-independent drift. — *DeltaN: EA 122, NVDA 250, MSFT 261, AMD 401+/-69, GOOG 729+/-183. Generation per build-up metaorder: 12k / 25k / 26k / 40k / 73k messages. Calibration month: Jan 2026 LOBSTER, held out of training.* (arXiv 2610.27100 Sec 2.1, 2.3, Eq.2-3, Table 2)
- [Certain] The blind control is what separates the model's own reaction from book mechanics. The build-up runs twice on the same windows and seeds. In the blind run the children fill in the simulator, but neither the message stream nor the book state fed to the model contains them. The control is needed because replay kernels with no model inside already identify delta=0.73-0.81 on AMD, inside the neural range, so the exponent alone cannot attribute the response. — *Replay kernels on AMD: delta 0.73-0.81. Neural v/b: >=3x in all 25 cells, >10x in 16.* (arXiv 2610.27100 Sec 2.1 (p.2))
- [Certain] Sample sizes are part of the protocol. Resolving a peak P at 3 standard errors takes n*=9*sigma1^2/P^2 rollouts per direction, with sigma1 taken from a pilot. Trading days, not rollouts, are the independent unit, and the bootstrap resamples whole days. A cell is printed only if its peak exceeds 3 SE and its law is identified. With fewer than 5 days, intervals look tight but contain the full-sample value only 40-68% of the time. — *1,024 rollouts per direction per phase over 20 trading days, about 1.4x the subsampled minimum. 95% day-clustered bootstrap half-widths on delta: 0.04-0.14.* (arXiv 2610.27100 Sec 2.1 (p.3), Table 3 caption)
- [Certain] The estimator is validated before it is read. (a) The identifiability test fits I=a*q^delta by least squares over a grid delta in [0.05,1.5] with step 0.01; a fit counts as not identified if |Y|<0.05 or the best delta sits on a grid bound. (b) The reported delta comes from a binned estimator with 18 quantile bins, signed conditional means and OLS through the positive means. (c) In planted-law tests on the replay's own point cloud, the estimator recovers delta in [0.5,1.0] with a downward bias of at most 0.11. (d) In 500 null trials it returned 0 false laws. (e) The historic replay, used as a negative control, identifies no law on any stock. — *Bias <=0.11 downward. False laws: 0/500 nulls. Binned delta moves <=0.05 across 6 volatility normalisations. kappa_P=sqrt(4 ln2)=1.665.* (arXiv 2610.27100 Sec 2.1 (p.3-4), Sec 3.4)
- [Certain] Distributional fidelity (LOB-Bench) is not a proxy for getting impact right, so the two must be gated separately. The correlation between the KS ranking and the |delta-0.5| ranking flips sign across stocks and is mostly negative within the neural family. The models' delta and Y rankings are nearly the same on every stock, which means the benchmark measures a component specific to each model. — *KS vs |delta-0.5| rank correlation: +0.54 on GOOG, -0.71 on MSFT (p=0.02). MSFT neural rows: -0.90 (p=0.08). Kendall W: 0.82 for delta, 0.81 for Y.* (arXiv 2610.27100 Sec 3.4)
- [Certain] Out-of-distribution failure shows up as a calibration error, not a shape error. On EA, the one stock outside the training corpus, the law is still identified but Y jumps an order of magnitude. A held-out asset therefore tests the amplitude Y, and a held-out month alone is not enough. — *Y in-corpus: AMD/MSFT about 2-9, GOOG 1.7-12.6, NVDA 7-19. Y on OOD EA: 12-50.* (arXiv 2610.27100 Sec 3.4, Table 3)
- [Certain] LOB-Bench measures distributional fidelity. Scalar aggregator functions Phi are compared with L1/TV distance (Freedman-Diaconis bins, width 2*IQR/n^(1/3)) and with Wasserstein-1 on mean-variance-normalised data. Conditional versions condition on 10 deciles of a second statistic, weighted by bucket mass. Divergence is tracked against the rollout step and thresholded at the 99th percentile of real-vs-real bootstrap L1. Further components: Eisler-2012 response functions over 6 event types with lags log-uniform in 1-200, scored as DeltaR = mean |R_real-R_gen|; a Conv1D+attention discriminator; aggregates reported as mean, median and IQM with bootstrapped 99% CIs. — *LOBS5 (35M params, up from 6.3M; 30.4 L40-GPU-days): discriminator ROC 0.83 on GOOG, DeltaR=2.45. RWKV-6 (170M): DeltaR=126. Baseline discriminator ROC about 1.* (arXiv 2502.09172 Sec 4, 4.1, 4.2, 5, App. E Fig 18)
- [Likely] C4 as published is a point-estimate rule. A defensible pass needs an equivalence test (TOST): at alpha=0.05, declare equivalence only if the 90% CI lies entirely inside [-SESOI,+SESOI]. With SESOI=0.10 on delta, the 90% day-clustered CI must sit inside [0.40,0.60]. Scaling the published 95% half-widths (0.04-0.14) by 1.645/1.96 gives 90% half-widths of about 0.034-0.118. A small model would then need |delta_hat-0.5| below roughly 0.07 at the tightest observed precision, and could not pass at the loosest. — *The 90% CI rule is Certain (source). The arithmetic 0.04*0.839=0.034 and 0.14*0.839=0.117 is mine, as is the inference about the threshold.* (Lakens 2017 primer (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5502906/); Lakens, Scheel & Isager 2018 AMPPS (https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/ampps/2515245918770963/); half-widths from arXiv 2610.27100 Table 3)
- [Likely] Financial-ML leakage controls. Purging drops training samples whose label or event windows overlap the test fold. An embargo adds a buffer after the test block. Combinatorial purged CV (CPCV) forms C(N,k) splits and recombines them into multiple backtest paths. Purging and embargo do not fix selection bias from trying many models; that needs multiple-testing corrections or forward tests. — *These are secondary sources; I did not read the book itself.* (López de Prado, Advances in Financial ML (2018) ch.7 and ch.12, via https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purged_cross-validation and https://www.interactivebrokers.com/campus/ibkr-quant-news/cross-validation-in-finance-purging-embargoing-combination/)
- [Certain] Probability of backtest overfitting (PBO), estimated by combinatorially symmetric cross-validation (CSCV), is the probability that the configuration that is best in-sample ranks below the median out-of-sample. The estimator is model-free and nonparametric. It applies directly to choosing a checkpoint by impact-gate score: the 'strategies' are checkpoints and the folds are day blocks. — *The definition is Certain. Applying it to checkpoints is my inference (Likely).* (Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado, Zhu, J. Computational Finance (DOI 10.21314/JCF.2016.322; https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2326253))
- [Likely] Adaptive overfitting from reusing a holdout is real but can be bounded. The Ladder releases a new leaderboard score only when a submission improves by more than a threshold, with guarantees in a fully adaptive setting. Shaky Ladder reaches leaderboard error O(n^-0.4). The Kaggle meta-analysis (>100 competitions) found little substantial adaptive overfitting, but those test sets had very large n. Here the effective n is about 20 trading days, so the risk of overfitting by choosing checkpoints on the test set is much higher. — *O(n^-0.4) vs the prior O(n^-1/3). The risk claim for n of about 20 days is my inference.* (Blum & Hardt ICML 2015 (arXiv 1502.04585); Hardt 2017 (arXiv 1706.02733); Roelofs et al. NeurIPS 2019 (https://papers.nips.cc/paper/9117-a-meta-analysis-of-overfitting-in-machine-learning))
- [Likely] Cross-asset leakage in crypto is concrete. Orders posted on BTC-USD move ETH-USD with much higher statistical significance than the reverse, so cross-asset spoofing is plausible. High-frequency lead-lag between bitcoin prices reaches up to 15 seconds. As a result, holding out an asset over the same dates as training does not give a clean test: concurrent BTC days leak ETH information. — *Lag of up to 15 s (thesis). Asymmetry of BTC->ETH significance: Certain from 2504.15908. The leakage implication is my inference.* (Fabre & Challet arXiv 2504.15908 Sec 2.3, Fig 8; CBS thesis (https://research-api.cbs.dk/ws/portalfiles/portal/59795067/612646_High_Frequency_Lead_Lag_Relationships_in_The_Bitcoin_Market.pdf))
- [Certain] There is no public labeled spoofing benchmark; the standard practice is synthetic injection into clean LOBSTER data. The 2508.17086 multilevel layering protocol works as follows. Start when bid/ask >= 1.0008. Place a bona-fide ask order 0-3 bps inside the touch, sized 2-3x the average order. Then place 10-15 non-bona-fide bid orders spanning levels 5 to 1, totalling 5-6x the average order, spaced 10-20 ms apart. Cancel them in a batch 100-500 ms after the fill. Metrics: AUC-PR (primary), AUROC, and F-beta with beta=4. — *Data: CSCO/TSLA/INTC, LOBSTER 2015-01-02. Anomaly ratio: train 0.03% (388/1.24M), validation 0.02%, test 5.04% (3,350/66,524). The test prevalence is inflated, so precision does not transfer to real base rates.* (arXiv 2508.17086 Sec 4.1, App A.3.1 Table 4, Table 6)
- [Certain] Real-data spoofing evaluation without labels: a model-based detector flags an order when the expected gain of a hypothetical spoofer is greater than 0. It is a 1-hidden-layer, 64-unit network, so it runs in real time. Its flag rate is very high, so precision at a fixed alert budget is the only meaningful operating metric. The earlier statistical-physics method validated on hand-inserted L3 spoof and layering orders (LUNA and BTC on Coinbase) and ranked intervals by the top-10 deviation. — *Dec 4-7 2024: 88,327,661 orders, 8,601,227 large (>=4,500 USD). 31% of large orders and 7% of all orders flagged. Suspicious orders at the top of book: 0% (large), 0.13% (all). Coinbase L3: 98.6% of open orders are cancelled.* (arXiv 2504.15908 Sec 3.3, Table 1; arXiv 2308.08683 Sec 6, Tables 1 and 5)
- [Likely] The cost of a campaign is dominated by rollouts, which favours small models. On AMD, one stock campaign is about 1,024 rollouts x 2 directions x 4 arms (free, visible, blind, relaxation) x about 40k messages, or roughly 3.3e8 generated messages. A smaller generator spends proportionally less compute per message, so for a fixed budget it can afford more stocks and more trading days, and days are the unit that buys precision. — *1024*2*4*40,000 = 327,680,000 messages per stock (AMD). Relaxation rollouts are shorter (i=10 plus 100 silent blocks), so this is an upper bound.* (Arm counts and lengths from arXiv 2610.27100 Sec 2.1 and Table 2; the arithmetic is mine)

**recommendations**

- GATE SPEC v0 for Almagest (5-20M params). G0 data hygiene: split by contiguous calendar blocks. Equities: train months, a 1-month validation block, a 1-month test block held out of training (as in LOB-Impact's Jan 2026), plus an embargo of at least 1 full trading day on each side of every boundary. That is longer than the longest metaorder (73k messages, under a day). Crypto (24/7): use UTC-day blocks with an embargo of at least 24h, and put ALL assets on the same calendar split. Never hold out an asset over concurrent dates, because BTC->ETH cross-impact and lead-lag up to 15 s leak information (2504.15908). In addition, keep at least 1 never-seen asset on test dates (EA-style) as the test for Y calibration.
- G1 LOB-Bench fidelity (cheap, run at every checkpoint): L1 and W1 IQM on the default scoring functions. The divergence-vs-horizon curve must stay under the 99th percentile of real-vs-real bootstrap L1 out to at least 4k messages. The response-function score DeltaR must be at or below LOBS5's 2.45 on the same stock. Use G1 only as a necessary filter, never for ranking checkpoints on impact (the KS vs |delta-0.5| rank correlation flips sign, 2610.27100).
- G2 Phase-1 balance (C1): |gamma_flow - gamma_replay| <= 0.10, |H - H_replay| <= 0.02, |free drift| <= 5 ticks over the build-up horizon. Also enforce H <= H_replay + 0.02, a no-free-lunch one-sided check, because 20 of 25 published neural rows have H above the stock's own value.
- G3 existence and attribution (C2, C3): the direct-L2 profile identifies a law (|Y| >= 0.05, delta not on a grid bound), the peak exceeds 3 SE, and the visible/blind ratio is >= 3 with its lower day-bootstrap 95% bound also >= 3. Pre-register a pilot to estimate sigma1, then size rollouts per direction by n* = 9*sigma1^2/P^2. Use at least 20 trading days and never fewer than 5 (with <5 days, CI coverage is only 40-68%).
- G4 square-root law, made stricter than the paper: (a) a bias-corrected delta, using planted-law calibration on the model's own point cloud (published bias <= 0.11), must pass TOST, meaning its 90% day-clustered bootstrap CI lies inside [0.40, 0.60]; (b) Y in [0.5, 1], with Y's 90% CI overlapping the band; (c) both must hold on at least 2 cohorts (low-OTR and high-OTR), and Y must lie within [0.25, 2] on the held-out asset. Report delta at the full 100-child metaorder only.
- G5 relaxation (C5): the relaxation-shape end-over-peak is at or below the OW kernel's value on the same stock and schedule (0.81 on AMD), the post-execution slope is <= 0 with its upper 95% day-bootstrap bound <= 0, and free drift < 20 ticks. Add a stretch target: end-over-peak <= 0.67, the real-market 2/3.
- G6 placebo battery, which must all return 'not identified' or a null within 3 SE: (i) the historic-replay negative control; (ii) 500 null trials of the estimator; (iii) sign-scrambled injections, where each child's side is randomised so the antisymmetric I should be 0; (iv) zero-size or far-from-touch injections, which should match the blind arm; (v) time-shifted conditioning windows, where the injection is aligned to a different day's context; (vi) a planted-law recovery check at delta in {0.5, 0.75, 1.0}. A model fails if any placebo identifies a law.
- G7 checkpoint-selection hygiene: choose checkpoints on the VALIDATION month only, using impact gates G2-G5. Touch the test month once per release, and log every test query. Put any leaderboard that is reused across iterations behind a Ladder-style threshold (Blum & Hardt), releasing a new score only if the gate statistic improves by more than its 95% half-width. Estimate PBO with CSCV over day blocks for the final K candidate checkpoints, and reject the selection if PBO > 0.5 (the median-rank criterion).
- G8 manipulation-detection eval: (a) inject synthetic layering using the 2508.17086 Table 4 parameters plus Cartea-style single-level spoofs, into TEST-month data only, at a realistic prevalence of <= 0.1% of windows (not their 5% test ratio); (b) the primary metric is precision at a fixed alert budget, e.g. top-K = 10 and top-K = 50 alerts per asset-day, with recall@budget and AUC-PR as secondary; (c) false-positive audit: alert rate on market-maker-style multi-level quoting injected as a hard negative; (d) use the model's own log-likelihood ratio or surprise as the detector, so no separate detector needs training, and compare it with a 64-unit spoofer-gain baseline (2504.15908).

**risks**

- A 20-trading-day test month makes the effective n for day-clustered inference about 20. TOST on delta with SESOI 0.10 may be underpowered for a small model unless rollouts per day are high. A realistic outcome is that the model fails TOST while the point estimate passes, so plan for 40 or more test days across months.
- The estimator's downward bias (<=0.11) is calibrated on replay clouds. A small model's point cloud may have a different noise structure, so the bias correction must be re-planted per model or the correction itself could create a spurious pass.
- Repeated use of LOB-Impact during development (stage-3 post-training on the impact law) turns the benchmark into a training signal. Gates must then be re-run on a month and asset never used for post-training, or the C4 and C5 passes are Goodharted.
- Crypto L3 feeds (e.g. Coinbase, where 98.6% of open orders are cancelled) have very different OTR and phi. DeltaN=9/phi can blow up the generation length per metaorder, making crypto campaigns far more expensive than NASDAQ ones.
- Synthetic spoofing injections are parametric and easy to detect. Detectors tuned on them may not transfer to real manipulation, and no public ground-truth labels exist (2308.08683 and 2504.15908 both note this).

**open_questions**

- Should the blind control also apply to the relaxation arm for a model with explicit slow memory? The paper skips it because the floor is 10x below the peak, but a two-clock model could leak the metaorder through its slow state.
- What SESOI is economically justified for Y, given execution-cost sensitivity? The paper's [0.5,1] band is empirical, not decision-theoretic.
- Does the LOB-Bench discriminator ROC (0.83 for 35M LOBS5) degrade gracefully for 5-20M models, or does it become the binding gate before impact does?
- Are there 2024-2026 labeled spoofing datasets from enforcement cases (e.g. Do & Putnins 2023, cited in 2504.15908) usable as a real-positive test set? Not verified here.

**recommendations**

- Make LOB-Impact C1-C5 (with the blind ratio) the primary acceptance test, and run it on NASDAQ via LOBSTER for direct comparison against the 55-120M panel. Target is S5-120M's AMD delta 0.66 or better and Y <= 1 at <=20M params.
- Implement the slow clock as an explicit bank of about 8-12 fixed-half-life leaky integrators of signed sqrt(V) trade flow (half-lives about 0.1 s to 1000 s, the 2603.24137 kernel family). Feed them to the per-event SSM as state features. This costs O(K) per event and adds roughly zero parameters, and it is the mechanism that gave QR/MDQR concavity and relaxation.
- Always feed the true book state from the JAX engine (best levels, queue sizes, spread) as model input instead of asking the model to infer it from messages. 2608.23706 shows implicit state tracking fails and degrades with state-space size.
- Start with an S5/LRU-style SSM backbone at about 5-20M. S5 is the least-bad family on exponent and amplitude in LOB-Impact, and longer context (4k) did not help, so spend budget on slow memory rather than on context length.
- Use synthetic causal pretraining (Stage 1) with Columbia-style compression and history-TV tests plus shared-noise counterfactual impact targets. Acero's <=32M synthetic-init result (2610.20104) suggests this transfers.
- Reposition novelty claims away from 'impact exponent near 0.5', which M3 already reports (0.53). Claim instead: first sub-30M order-level generator to pass C4 (delta and Y in [0.5,1]) and C5 (relaxation), with visible/blind attribution, on NASDAQ and crypto L3.
- For the post-training stage, use the LOB-Impact estimator (or a differentiable proxy: binned impact vs q, end/peak ratio) as an ES/EGGROLL fitness on a small model. Zeroth-order ES is cheapest at small parameter counts.
- For crypto, size the slow memory in seconds, not messages: BTC/USD L3 runs a median of about 199 events/s, so a 1024-token context is about 5 s (2611.14117). Without explicit slow state, expect the deep-sweep runaway that paper observed.
- Move early on manipulation detection (counterfactual likelihood or impact-residual scoring with the generator). No generative spoofing detector is published yet, but Tampere's funded project explicitly targets it.

**risks**

- Oxford BOLD owns the benchmark and sigma0 and may publish a slow-memory or replenishment fix (or release checkpoints) before us; their open questions already list hidden liquidity and cancellation modelling.
- M3 already has public code and weights including a ~10M variant. Someone could run LOB-Impact on M3-tiny and claim a small-model result first.
- Y and relaxation targets are calibrated on equities. Crypto empirical Y/relaxation references are thinner, so a crypto-only pass may be contested.
- Explicitly engineered propagator features could be criticised as 'baking in' the answer. The blind-control ratio and C1 balance (H, gamma_flow) are needed to show the model's own reaction, not a hard-coded kernel.
- Evaluation cost: about 1,024 rollouts per direction per phase over 20 days at 12k-73k messages each is heavy even for small models, so a fast JAX engine plus a batched recurrent model is mandatory.
