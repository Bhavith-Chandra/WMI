# Theory for adapting when hidden disturbances change over time (piecewise-stationary / change-point) and for hierarchical replanning under disturbances

Verification status: items marked [verified] were checked this session against the arXiv/PMLR abstract or full text. Items marked [recalled] are standard results I know well, with the citation and ID given from memory. The report writer should treat [recalled] constants and log factors as approximate. Notation: T = horizon; K / A = number of arms or actions; L = Υ_T = number of change points (switches); V_T / B_T / Δ = total variation budget; d = linear dimension.

## Q1. Piecewise-stationary bandits and linear bandits: exact rates for forgetting and restarting

### Takeaway
With L abrupt switches, the minimax dynamic regret is Θ̃(√(K L T)) for K-armed bandits. A planner that knows L reaches √(L T) up to log factors by forgetting at a fixed rate: a discount 1 − γ ≈ √(L/T) or a window τ ≈ √(T log T / L). Change-detection-and-restart reaches the same rate (CUSUM-UCB, GLR-klUCB). Since 2019, adaptive algorithms (AdSwitch, MASTER) reach √(L T) without knowing L. A planner that never forgets, such as standard UCB with logarithmic stationary regret, provably suffers Ω(T / log T) regret once a breakpoint occurs. That is essentially linear regret. Under a variation budget B_T instead of a switch count, the rate becomes T^{2/3}: (K log K)^{1/3} V_T^{1/3} T^{2/3} for bandits and d^{2/3} B_T^{1/3} T^{2/3} for linear bandits.

### Cited Findings
**Garivier & Moulines, "On upper-confidence bound policies for switching bandit problems" (ALT 2011; arXiv:0805.3415)** [verified from full text]
- Setting: K arms, abruptly changing reward distributions, piecewise i.i.d. between unknown breakpoints, Υ_T breakpoints up to T. — [arXiv:0805.3415](https://arxiv.org/abs/0805.3415)
- Discounted UCB (Thm 1, Remark 3): with γ = 1 − (4B)^{-1}√(Υ_T/T), the expected number of suboptimal plays is O(√(T Υ_T) log T). With Υ_T = O(T^β), the regret is O(T^{(1+β)/2} log T). This requires knowing T and the growth of Υ_T. A doubling trick can replace knowledge of T only. — [ar5iv 0805.3415](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/0805.3415)
- Sliding-window UCB (Thm 7, Remark 9): for a window τ, E[Ñ_T(i)] ≤ C(τ)·T log τ/τ + τ Υ_T + log²τ. With τ = 2B√(T log T / Υ_T), this gives O(√(Υ_T T log T)), slightly better than D-UCB (√log T instead of log T). It requires knowing T and Υ_T. — [ar5iv 0805.3415](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/0805.3415)
- Lower bound (Thm 13 / Cor 14): for any policy, max{stationary regret, regret with a breakpoint} ≥ √(C(μ) T). No policy achieves non-stationary regret below order √T, and the bound holds even with a single breakpoint. — [ar5iv 0805.3415](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/0805.3415)
- **Never-forgetting planners suffer near-linear regret (Remark 16):** any policy with E[N_T(K)] = Θ(log T) in the stationary case, such as standard UCB, has regret ≥ c·T / log T once breakpoints are present. In the paper's words, a logarithmic policy "cannot reach a regret of order smaller than T/log T in presence of breakpoints." This trade-off between stationary regret and non-stationary regret is the formal case for forgetting. — [ar5iv 0805.3415](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/0805.3415)

**Lower bound Ω(√(K L T))**
- The Garivier–Moulines Ω(√T) bound does not depend on the number of changes or the number of arms. Later work states a tighter minimax lower bound of Ω(√(N K T)), with N segments and K arms, for piecewise-stationary MAB and combinatorial semi-bandits. — [Zhou et al., arXiv:1908.10402](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.10402)
- For piecewise-stationary cascading bandits, the minimax lower bound is Ω(√(N L T)). — [arXiv:1909.05886](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1909.05886)
- A 2025 survey-style paper calls the Garivier–Moulines bound "loose" because it does not depend on N_T. It cites Ω(√(N_T A T)) for a subclass of piecewise-stationary MABs and Õ(√(N_T T)) order-optimal upper bounds for detection-augmented procedures. — [arXiv:2501.01291](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.01291)
- [recalled] The √(S K T) scaling for S switches also appears in the adversarial setting. EXP3.S (Auer, Cesa-Bianchi, Freund, Schapire, SIAM J. Computing 32(1), 2002) has regret Õ(√(S K T)) against the best S-switch sequence when S is known. — [SIAM J. Comput. 2002](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/S0097539701398375)

**Change detection plus restart**
- [recalled] Liu, Lee & Shroff, "A change-detection based framework for piecewise-stationary multi-armed bandit problem," AAAI 2018 (arXiv:1711.03539). CUSUM-UCB and PHT-UCB run a two-sided CUSUM detector per arm and reset that arm's statistics when an alarm fires. They add uniform forced exploration at rate α. Regret is O(√(T M log(T/M))) when the number of change points M is known (used to tune α and the detector thresholds). The analysis assumes a minimum change magnitude and a minimum segment length. — [arXiv:1711.03539](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.03539)
- Besson, Kaufmann, Maillard & Seznec, "Efficient change-point detection for tackling piecewise-stationary bandits" (arXiv:1902.01575, v2 Dec 2020; published JMLR 2022 [recalled venue]). GLR-klUCB pairs kl-UCB with a parameter-free Bernoulli GLR change-point detector, in global-restart and local (per-arm) restart variants. Regret is O(√(T A Υ_T log T)) on "easy" instances, i.e., when changes are large enough relative to segment length to be detectable. The abstract says the algorithm needs no prior knowledge of the arm means. [verified from abstract] [recalled, to check in the paper] The tuning that reaches the √(Υ_T) dependence uses knowledge of Υ_T to set the forced-exploration rate. — [arXiv:1902.01575](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.01575)

**Adaptive without knowing the number of switches**
- Auer, Gajane & Ortner, "Adaptively tracking the best bandit arm with an unknown number of distribution changes," COLT 2019, PMLR 99:138–158. AdSwitch gets nearly minimax-optimal regret without knowing the number of changes, described as the first optimal bound for an algorithm not tuned to that number. [recalled] The COLT paper is for 2 arms, with the K-arm extension in the joint note by Auer, Chen, Gajane, Lee, Luo, Ortner & Wei, "Achieving optimal dynamic regret for non-stationary bandits without prior information," COLT 2019, PMLR 99:159–163. — [PMLR v99 auer19a](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v99/auer19a.html)
- Suk & Kpotufe describe the Auer et al. result as the first adaptive procedure guaranteeing optimal dynamic regret √(L T) for unknown L. They refine it to count only "significant" arm switches. Suk & Kpotufe, "Tracking most significant arm switches in bandits," COLT 2022, arXiv:2112.13838. — [arXiv:2112.13838](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.13838)
- [recalled] Chen, Lee, Luo & Wei, "A new algorithm for non-stationary contextual bandits: efficient, optimal and parameter-free," COLT 2019 (arXiv:1902.00980). It gets Õ(√(L T)) and Õ(Δ^{1/3} T^{2/3}) for contextual bandits without knowing L or Δ. — [arXiv:1902.00980](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.00980)

**Variation-budget setting (drift instead of switches)**
- [recalled] Besbes, Gur & Zeevi, "Stochastic multi-armed-bandit problem with non-stationary rewards," NeurIPS 2014 (arXiv:1405.3316). With total variation V_T known, Rexp3 with restarts gets O((K log K)^{1/3} V_T^{1/3} T^{2/3}). They prove a matching lower bound Ω((K V_T)^{1/3} T^{2/3}). — [arXiv:1405.3316](https://arxiv.org/abs/1405.3316)
- Russac, Vernade & Cappé, "Weighted linear bandits for non-stationary environments," NeurIPS 2019 (arXiv:1909.09146). D-LinUCB uses exponentially discounted weighted least squares, the linear analogue of D-UCB. Its dynamic regret is of order d^{2/3} B_T^{1/3} T^{2/3}, with B_T measuring parameter variation. The guarantees cover both slowly varying and abruptly changing environments. [recalled] The discount is tuned with knowledge of B_T. — [arXiv:1909.09146](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.09146)
- [recalled] Cheung, Simchi-Levi & Zhu, "Learning to optimize under non-stationarity," AISTATS 2019 (arXiv:1810.03024). The journal version is "Hedging the drift: learning to optimize under nonstationarity," Management Science 68(3), 2022. SW-UCB for linear bandits claims Õ(d^{2/3} B_T^{1/3} T^{2/3}) with B_T known. The Bandits-over-Bandits (BOB) wrapper claims Õ(d^{2/3} B_T^{1/4} T^{3/4}) with B_T unknown. — [arXiv:1810.03024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.03024v5)
- **Correction in this literature:** Zhao, Zhang, Jiang & Zhou, "A simple approach for non-stationary linear bandits," AISTATS 2020, PMLR 108:746–755, use restarting and claim Õ(d^{2/3}(1+P_T)^{1/3} T^{2/3}), matching an Ω(d^{2/3}(1+P_T)^{1/3} T^{2/3}) minimax lower bound. Their arXiv note (2103.05324) says the conference version relied on a wrong argument (Section 4.3, eq. 20) and fixes it. [recalled] The same technical gap affects the sliding-window and weighted analyses of Cheung et al. and Russac et al. Corrected rates without extra assumptions are weaker; the commonly cited corrected order for sliding-window or restarting LinUCB is Õ(d^{7/8} B_T^{1/4} T^{3/4}). — [arXiv:2103.05324](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.05324v2); [PMLR v108](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v108/zhao20a.html)
- Generalized linear extension: Russac et al., "Algorithms for non-stationary generalized linear bandits" (arXiv:2003.10113). — [arXiv:2003.10113](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.10113)

### Inferences
- In this literature, "how a hidden-parameter planner should forget" has a precise answer. Keep an effective memory of length ≈ √(T/L) when switches are abrupt and L is known, or ≈ (T/B_T)^{2/3}-type windows under drift. Alternatively, restart on a detected change. Infinite memory gives Ω(T/log T) regret. Zero memory (never adapting, never using the inferred parameter) gives linear regret against the dynamic oracle whenever arms differ by a constant gap.
- Restart-on-detection gives the tightest constants when changes are large and segments are long. Passive forgetting (discounting or windows) needs no detectability assumption but pays the extra log factors and is tuned to L.
- Not knowing L costs nothing in the order of T (√(L T) is still achievable), but it needs more elaborate machinery (AdSwitch, MASTER) than a fixed window.

### Gaps
- I did not confirm in the full text the exact constants and assumptions of Liu–Lee–Shroff, including the minimum gap and minimum segment length conditions. They are given from memory.
- I did not confirm the exact corrected rate for Cheung et al. and Russac et al. after the Zhao et al. flaw (d^{7/8} B^{1/4} T^{3/4} is from memory).
- I found no single canonical paper whose headline theorem is exactly "Ω(√(K L T)) for piecewise-stationary K-armed stochastic bandits." The bound is usually attributed via EXP3.S-style constructions or to Seznec et al.; the citations above are the closest verified statements.

## Q2. Bayesian online change-point detection, surprise-triggered resets, and the detection-delay vs false-alarm trade-off

### Takeaway
Classical quickest change detection gives exact optimality for a single change between known pre- and post-change distributions. Page's CUSUM is exactly optimal (Moustakides 1986) for Lorden's worst-case detection delay subject to a false-alarm constraint (mean time between false alarms ≥ γ). Its delay is ≈ log γ / KL(post‖pre). Bayesian online change-point detection (Adams & MacKay 2007) is the standard Bayesian run-length filter, a principled "surprise-triggered reset." It has no regret guarantees when used inside a bandit or RL planner. That is a gap.

### Cited Findings
- [recalled] Page (1954), "Continuous inspection schemes," Biometrika 41:100–115. It introduced CUSUM: S_t = max(0, S_{t−1} + log(p_1(x_t)/p_0(x_t))), alarm when S_t ≥ h. — [Biometrika 1954](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/41.1-2.100)
- [recalled] Lorden (1971), "Procedures for reacting to a change in distribution," Annals of Mathematical Statistics 42(6):1897–1908. It defines the worst-worst-case detection delay sup_ν ess sup E_ν[(τ − ν + 1)^+ | F_{ν−1}] subject to E_∞[τ] ≥ γ. It shows CUSUM is asymptotically optimal as γ → ∞, with delay ~ log γ / I, where I = KL(p_1‖p_0). — [Ann. Math. Stat. 1971](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/annals-of-mathematical-statistics/volume-42/issue-6/Procedures-for-Reacting-to-a-Change-in-Distribution/10.1214/aoms/1177693055.full)
- [recalled] Moustakides (1986), "Optimal stopping times for detecting changes in distributions," Annals of Statistics 14(4):1379–1387. It proves CUSUM is exactly (non-asymptotically) optimal for Lorden's criterion. — [Ann. Stat. 1986](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/annals-of-statistics/volume-14/issue-4/Optimal-Stopping-Times-for-Detecting-Changes-in-Distributions/10.1214/aos/1176350164.full)
- [recalled] Lai (1998), "Information bounds and quick detection of parameter changes in stochastic systems," IEEE Trans. Information Theory 44(7). It extends the log γ / I asymptotic lower bound to non-i.i.d. settings and to the probability-of-false-alarm-in-a-window criterion. It also gives window-limited GLR procedures for unknown post-change parameters, which is the relevant case when the post-switch hidden parameter is unknown. — [IEEE TIT 1998](https://doi.org/10.1109/18.737522)
- [recalled] The Bayesian counterpart is Shiryaev's procedure (geometric prior on the change time), optimal for average detection delay plus a cost on false alarms. Pollak (1985) gives the minimax variant based on Shiryaev–Roberts.
- [recalled] Adams & MacKay (2007), "Bayesian online changepoint detection," arXiv:0710.3742. It maintains a posterior over the run length r_t (time since the last change) using a hazard function H(r) (constant H = 1/λ for geometric gaps) and a conjugate predictive model per run length. The recursion is P(r_t, x_{1:t}) = Σ_{r_{t−1}} P(r_t | r_{t−1}) P(x_t | r_{t−1}, x^{(r)}) P(r_{t−1}, x_{1:t−1}). Its cost is O(t) per step, made constant with pruning. Mass on r_t = 0 is a soft "reset" driven by the predictive surprise of x_t. — [arXiv:0710.3742](https://arxiv.org/abs/0710.3742)
- [recalled] Mellor & Shapiro, "Thompson sampling in switching environments with Bayesian online change detection," AISTATS 2013 (arXiv:1302.3721). It combines BOCPD with Thompson sampling and gives empirical results only, with no regret bound. — [arXiv:1302.3721](https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.3721)
- The bandit change-detection methods in Q1 (CUSUM-UCB, GLR-klUCB) are the setting where "detect, then reset" does come with regret guarantees. The detector's false-alarm probability and detection delay enter the regret bound directly. — [arXiv:1902.01575](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.01575)

### Inferences
- A planner that infers a hidden disturbance θ from outcome residuals can run CUSUM or GLR on the residual log-likelihood ratio between the current θ estimate and alternatives. The threshold h ≈ log γ trades the false-alarm interval γ against delay ≈ h / KL. In a bandit-style regret decomposition, each change costs about delay × gap, and each false alarm costs a re-exploration burn-in. Setting γ polynomial in T keeps false alarms o(1) and gives the √(L T)-type total.
- A BOCPD-style soft reset is the Bayesian analogue of this. Treating it as a "surprise-triggered reset" is principled, but its regret properties are unproven in planning loops.

### Gaps
- No regret bound exists for BOCPD-driven or "surprise-triggered" resets inside bandit or RL planners. I found only empirical work (Mellor & Shapiro 2013 and successors). This is a real theory gap.
- Detection-delay optimality results assume known pre- and post-change distributions (CUSUM) or parametric families (GLR). Theory for learned world-model residuals is absent.

## Q3. Non-stationary RL and adaptive control with switching dynamics: regret bounds

### Takeaway
For MDPs with L abrupt changes or variation budgets on rewards and transitions, sliding-window or restart variants of UCRL2 get Õ(L^{1/3} T^{2/3}) (known L), with SWUCRL2-CW at Õ(D_max(B_r+B_p)^{1/4} S^{2/3} A^{1/2} T^{3/4}). The black-box MASTER reduction (Wei & Luo, COLT 2021) gets the optimal Õ(min{√(L T), Δ^{1/3} T^{2/3}}) without prior knowledge, for any base algorithm with optimal stationary regret. Episodic model-free RestartQ-UCB is minimax-optimal in Δ^{1/3} T^{2/3} up to an H^{1/3} factor.

### Cited Findings
- [recalled] Jaksch, Ortner & Auer, "Near-optimal regret bounds for reinforcement learning," JMLR 11:1563–1600, 2010. UCRL2 with periodic restarts in MDPs whose rewards and transitions may change l times gets Õ(l^{1/3} T^{2/3} D S √A). This is the first switching-MDP bound. — [JMLR 2010](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v11/jaksch10a.html)
- [recalled] Gajane, Ortner & Auer, "A sliding-window algorithm for Markov decision processes with arbitrarily changing rewards and transitions" (arXiv:1805.10066). SW-UCRL gets Õ(l^{1/3} T^{2/3}) with l known. — [arXiv:1805.10066](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.10066)
- Cheung, Simchi-Levi & Zhu, "Reinforcement learning for non-stationary Markov decision processes: the blessing of (more) optimism," ICML 2020 (arXiv:2006.14389). The journal version is arXiv:1906.02922. Setting: undiscounted, with variation budgets B_r and B_p. SWUCRL2-CW needs the budgets known. BORL (bandit-over-RL) tunes it and gets the same bound without them. — [arXiv:2006.14389](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.14389)
  - Theorem 4.3: with W* = S^{2/3} A^{1/2} T^{1/2} (B_r+B_p)^{-1/2} and η* = √(B_p W*/T), Dyn-Reg_T = Õ(D_max (B_r+B_p)^{1/4} S^{2/3} A^{1/2} T^{3/4}). — [arXiv:1906.02922 v4](https://arxiv.org/html/1906.02922v4)
  - Confidence widening (η) is needed because sliding-window estimates under drift can yield optimistic MDPs with huge diameter. Widening the transition confidence set keeps the diameter bounded, which is a non-trivial way that non-stationarity interacts with planning. — [arXiv:1906.02922 v4](https://arxiv.org/html/1906.02922v4)
  - The paper gives no non-stationary MDP lower bound. It notes only that the bound matches Besbes et al. in the single-state bandit case. — [arXiv:1906.02922 v4](https://arxiv.org/html/1906.02922v4)
- Wei & Luo, "Non-stationary reinforcement learning without prior knowledge: an optimal black-box approach," COLT 2021 (arXiv:2102.05406). MASTER is a reduction: any base algorithm with optimal regret in (near-)stationary environments gives dynamic regret Õ(min{√(L T), Δ^{1/3} T^{2/3}}) without knowing L or Δ, "in most cases." It covers contextual and multi-armed bandits, linear and generalized linear bandits (improving prior bounds), episodic MDPs and infinite-horizon MDPs. [recalled] The mechanism runs base-algorithm instances at multiple scales on randomized schedules and restarts when stationarity tests on observed performance fail. — [arXiv:2102.05406](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.05406)
- [recalled] Mao, Zhang, Zhu, Simchi-Levi & Başar, "Near-optimal model-free reinforcement learning in non-stationary episodic MDPs," ICML 2021 (arXiv:2010.03161). RestartQ-UCB gets Õ(S^{1/3} A^{1/3} Δ^{1/3} H T^{2/3}), with a lower bound of Ω(S^{1/3} A^{1/3} Δ^{1/3} H^{2/3} T^{2/3}). — [arXiv:2010.03161](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.03161)
- [recalled] Domingues, Ménard, Pirotta, Kaufmann & Valko, "A kernel-based approach to non-stationary reinforcement learning in metric spaces," AISTATS 2021 (arXiv:2007.05078). KeRNS combines kernel smoothing in state-action space with time-decaying weights (a kernel analogue of discounting). It gives dynamic-regret bounds for episodic MDPs in metric spaces, with rates depending on the covering dimension and the variation budget. — [arXiv:2007.05078](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.05078)
- Options-UCBVI's analysis explicitly handles non-stationary (time-inhomogeneous) transition models in finite-horizon semi-MDPs. That is non-stationary in h, not drifting over episodes. — [arXiv:2406.15124](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.15124)

### Inferences
- The bandit picture carries over to RL: forgetting (windows or restarts) plus extra optimism gives sublinear dynamic regret. Without prior knowledge, MASTER is the general recipe: a multi-scale restart test on observed returns, which is a "hindsight-style" consistency check.
- For a planner that infers a disturbance parameter θ, these results apply if θ-switches show up as changes in rewards or transitions. L counts switches and Δ counts total parameter drift in the chosen metric.

### Gaps
- For continuous-state adaptive control (LQR or LQG with switching or Markov-jump dynamics, piecewise-constant disturbances), I did not verify a dynamic-regret result this session. [recalled] There is work on online LQR with time-varying or adversarial disturbances, e.g., non-stochastic control (Agarwal, Bullins, Hazan, Kakade & Singh, ICML 2019, arXiv:1902.08721). Its regret is against the best fixed disturbance-action policy, not against a switching oracle. Mapping switching-dynamics adaptive control onto √(L T) is left as a gap.
- There is no lower bound for SWUCRL2-CW's T^{3/4} rate in the variation-budget MDP case. The MASTER and RestartQ T^{2/3} rates suggest T^{3/4} is not tight.

## Q4. Hierarchical planning and subgoal re-optimization under disturbances, and whether hierarchy plus adaptation provably beats either alone

### Takeaway
Hierarchical deep RL systems are empirical and give no robustness or regret guarantees under exogenous disturbances. These include FeUdal Networks, HIRO, HAC, Director and LeCun's H-JEPA proposal, which re-plan subgoals at a coarse timescale. In HRL, "non-stationarity" usually means the low-level policy changing during training, not exogenous switching. Theory exists in three separate strands:
- regret for learning with options or hierarchies in stationary MDPs, with conditions under which options reduce regret;
- recursive-feasibility and robust-constraint guarantees for hierarchical or tube MPC with bounded disturbances, which are not regret bounds;
- provable meta-RL that recovers a hierarchy and then exploits it on a new task.

I found no theorem showing that hierarchy combined with change-point adaptation (forgetting or restarting) gets better dynamic regret than flat adaptive or hierarchical non-adaptive planners. This is a valuable negative finding.

### Cited Findings
**Empirical hierarchical systems (no theory under disturbances)**
- [recalled] Vezhnevets et al., "FeUdal networks for hierarchical reinforcement learning," ICML 2017 (arXiv:1703.01161). A Manager sets directional goals in a latent space at a lower temporal resolution and a Worker executes them. — [arXiv:1703.01161](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.01161)
- [recalled] Nachum, Gu, Lee & Levine, "Data-efficient hierarchical reinforcement learning (HIRO)," NeurIPS 2018 (arXiv:1805.08296). The high level proposes state-space subgoals every c steps. An off-policy correction relabels past high-level actions with the subgoal that maximizes the likelihood of the low-level actions actually taken. This is a hindsight correction for the non-stationarity of the lower level. — [arXiv:1805.08296](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.08296)
- [recalled] Levy, Konidaris, Platt & Saenko, "Learning multi-level hierarchies with hindsight (HAC)," ICLR 2019 (arXiv:1712.00948). Hindsight action and goal transitions make each level's training stationary by pretending the lower level is optimal. — [arXiv:1712.00948](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.00948)
- [recalled] Hafner, Lee, Fischer & Abbeel, "Deep hierarchical planning from pixels (Director)," NeurIPS 2022 (arXiv:2206.04114). A manager picks latent goals every K = 8 steps inside a learned world model (DreamerV2), and the worker reaches them. It is empirical only. — [arXiv:2206.04114](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.04114)
- [recalled] LeCun, "A path towards autonomous machine intelligence," v0.9.2, OpenReview, June 2022. It proposes H-JEPA, a hierarchical joint-embedding predictive world model. High levels predict abstract long-horizon states and set subgoals or costs for lower levels. Planning is MPC-style and re-optimizes at each level ("Mode-2"). It is a position paper with no theorems. — [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=BZ5a1r-kVsf)

- [recalled] Fruit, Pirotta, Lazaric & Brunskill, "Regret minimization in MDPs with options without prior knowledge," NeurIPS 2017. FSUCRL removes the need to know option-duration and reward distributions. — [NeurIPS 2017](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/2017/hash/90599c8fdd2f6e7a03ad173e2f535751-Abstract.html)
- Option-dependent analysis for finite-horizon SMDPs: a regret upper bound for a two-level hierarchy, arguing that options shorten the effective planning horizon and that HRL from scratch can beat flat methods in some settings (arXiv:2305.06936). The follow-up Options-UCBVI bounds regret in terms of the number of options played per episode (arXiv:2406.15124, RLC 2024). — [arXiv:2305.06936](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.06936); [arXiv:2406.15124](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.15124)
- [recalled] Wen, Precup, Ibrahimi, Barreto, Van Roy & Singh, "On efficiency in hierarchical reinforcement learning," NeurIPS 2020. It gives statistical and computational efficiency bounds when an MDP decomposes into repeated, equivalent subMDPs. — [NeurIPS 2020](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2020/hash/4a5cfa9281924139db466a8a19291aff-Abstract.html)
- [recalled] Nachum, Gu, Lee & Levine, "Near-optimal representation learning for hierarchical reinforcement learning," ICLR 2019 (arXiv:1810.01257). It bounds the sub-optimality of goal-conditioned hierarchies induced by a learned goal representation. — [arXiv:1810.01257](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.01257)
- Chua, Lei & Lee, "Provable hierarchy-based meta-reinforcement learning," AISTATS 2023, PMLR 206:10918–10967 (arXiv:2110.09507). In a tabular setting, latent hierarchical structure is learned during meta-training under "diversity conditions," and regret bounds are given for a learner exploiting the recovered hierarchy on a meta-test task. This is the closest existing result to "hierarchy + adaptation," but the adaptation is across tasks, not within-task switches. — [PMLR v206](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v206/chua23a.html); [arXiv:2110.09507](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.09507)
- In decentralized hierarchical multi-agent MDPs, one agent's changing policy makes another's environment non-stationary. Naive compensation can cause an exponential blowup in the regret bound (arXiv:2111.00781). — [arXiv:2111.00781](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.00781)

**Hierarchical MPC under bounded disturbances (feasibility and stability, not regret)**
- Kögel, Ibrahim, Kallies & Findeisen, "Safe hierarchical model predictive control and planning for autonomous systems" (arXiv:2203.14269). A planner layer re-optimizes over a moving horizon, and a lower cyclic-horizon tube MPC rejects bounded disturbances. The paper derives recursive-feasibility conditions that guarantee constraint satisfaction across control modes and disturbances. — [arXiv:2203.14269](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.14269)
- Surma & Jamshidnejad, "Recursive feasibility without terminal constraints via parent–child MPC architecture" (arXiv:2507.10166; Results in Control and Optimization, 2026). Parent layers supply robust invariant tubes that replace terminal constraints, giving stability and feasibility guarantees. They report up to 8× lower solve time than standard robust tube MPC. — [arXiv:2507.10166](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.10166v1)
- Aghaei, Daeichian & Puig, hierarchical decentralized reference governor (arXiv:2012.10250). Receding-horizon reference (subgoal) governing uses dynamic constraint tightening. The paper proves feasibility, stability, convergence and robust constraint satisfaction. Under bounded unmeasured disturbances, command-governor schemes govern the reference with respect to the nominal model and minimal robust positively invariant sets. — [arXiv:2012.10250](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2012.10250)
- [recalled] Scattolini, "Architectures for distributed and hierarchical model predictive control – a review," Journal of Process Control 19(5):723–731, 2009. It is the standard survey of multi-rate and hierarchical MPC, where upper layers re-optimize setpoints or subgoals slowly and lower layers track them quickly. — [J. Process Control 2009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jprocont.2009.02.003)

### Inferences
- Hierarchical MPC theory guarantees that subgoal re-optimization stays feasible and stable under bounded disturbances. It does not address learning the disturbance or regret against a switching oracle.
- Option or hierarchy regret theory shows that hierarchy can cut regret by shortening the effective horizon or sharing structure, but only in stationary MDPs.
- Non-stationary RL theory (MASTER, SWUCRL2-CW, RestartQ) is flat.
- A natural, unproven conjecture combines them. If a hidden disturbance switch affects only low-level dynamics, restarting only the low-level learner (a "local restart," analogous to GLR-klUCB's per-arm restarts) while keeping high-level subgoal structure could replace the global √(L T · complexity) with √(L T · low-level complexity). Similarly, MASTER applied per level would give hierarchical dynamic-regret bounds. No paper I found proves this.
- In HRL papers, "non-stationarity" (HIRO, HAC) usually refers to the changing lower-level policy during training. Their hindsight relabeling is a mechanism for stationarizing the high level, not for exogenous disturbance switches. The report should not conflate the two.

### Gaps
- **No theory found** for dynamic regret of hierarchical or subgoal replanning under piecewise-stationary or switching disturbances.
- **No theory found** showing hierarchy + adaptation strictly beats adaptive-flat or hierarchical-non-adaptive planners. The closest are Chua, Lei & Lee 2023 (meta-RL across tasks) and the option-regret papers (stationary).
- **No theory found** for HIRO, Director, FeUdal or H-JEPA robustness under exogenous disturbances; these are empirical or position works.
- I did not search exhaustively for robust or adaptive hierarchical MPC with online parameter estimation under switching (e.g., adaptive tube MPC with set-membership estimation inside a hierarchy). There may be feasibility results there, but I expect no regret results.
