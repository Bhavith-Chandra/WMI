# Persistence decides when hindsight planning wins

The theory already supports a precise answer. Planning that conditions on observed outcomes ("hindsight") can never do worse than outcome-ignoring forward planning at the Bayes optimum. This follows from policy-class inclusion and Blackwell's theorem. It is strictly better exactly when (i) some hidden quantity persists across the decisions being compared, (ii) the observed outcomes carry information about the decision-relevant part of that quantity, and (iii) the best action actually changes across that quantity's plausible values. When all three hold, the gap is not a constant factor but a difference in growth rate. An outcome-ignoring planner pays **Θ(T)·EVPI** regret against a parameter-clairvoyant. A hindsight planner pays only a learning cost: **O(d log T)** when the disturbance is identified "for free" from residuals (additive offsets, the A-unknown linear-quadratic regulator (LQR) case), or **Θ̃(√T)** when identification needs the actions themselves to probe (gain faults, the A-and-B-unknown LQR case). That cost becomes **√(LT)** under L abrupt switches, and the gap turns near-linear the other way if the planner never forgets. In control engineering, offset-free MPC and the steady-state Kalman filter give exact, assumption-explicit versions of the same claim. "Residual reuse" is the Kalman-gain-1 limit, which is optimal only when disturbance innovation dominates measurement noise. For latent/JEPA world models, the anchor result is the November 2026 Counterfactual World Models paper. It proves factual world models cannot identify individual counterfactuals, shows residual reuse is the unique rule exact in additive worlds, and bounds its error at a minimax-optimal O(h²) relative risk. It also shows empirically that one-step hindsight planning beats an outcome-blind true-simulator oracle (35–69% vs 14–23% success). But **no paper proves a decision-level (regret or suboptimality) theorem for hindsight planning with a learned latent world model**. Multi-step chaining, switching disturbances, gain/contact faults and the link between test-time adaptation and residual reuse are all open. That is the gap a new paper can fill.

## Inclusion, Blackwell and EVPI fix the sign and size of the advantage

The weakest and most robust statement is a feasibility argument, and it is the one a paper should lead with. Let open-loop (outcome-ignoring) policies be maps from time and pre-outcome information to actions, and let hindsight policies be maps from the full history H_t. The first class is contained in the second. So for any prior, horizon and reward, the Bayes-optimal hindsight value V*_H is at least the open-loop value V*_OL, provided expectations are taken under the planner's own law. **Blackwell's informativeness theorem** strengthens this from "signal vs no signal" to an ordering of signals. For experiments P and Q, Q is a garbling of P (Q = P·M for a Markov kernel M) iff P gives at least the Bayes value of Q in every finite decision problem under every prior ([Wikipedia: Blackwell's informativeness theorem](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackwell%27s_informativeness_theorem); [QuantEcon](https://python.quantecon.org/blackwell_kihlstrom.html); [Blackwell 1953](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/annals-of-mathematical-statistics/volume-24/issue-2/Equivalent-Comparisons-of-Experiments/10.1214/aoms/1177729032.short)). Applied to the hindsight setting, retaining the full transition (s_t, a_t, s_{t+1}) is weakly better than retaining any summary of it. That gives a formal basis for comparing, say, a full latent residual against a scalar surprise signal.

The sequential version of the same fact is convexity of the value function in the belief. **Smallwood and Sondik** proved that the finite-horizon optimal POMDP value is piecewise-linear and convex in the belief ([INFORMS](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/fpi/10.1287/opre.21.5.1071)). Augment the state with the static hidden parameter θ; then each observed transition yields a posterior that is a martingale around the prior, and Jensen's inequality stage by stage shows that acting on the updated belief cannot lose. **Howard's value of information** then gives the size of the gap ([Scilit](https://www.scilit.net/publications/6df64dca9e2dcb60dd51e3a0eb6a9aa1)). The per-step loss of an outcome-ignoring planner against a θ-clairvoyant is the one-step expected value of perfect information, **Δ_OL = E_θ[max_a μ(a,θ)] − max_a E_θ[μ(a,θ)]**. It is strictly positive unless a single action is optimal for almost every θ. In a stationary problem the outcome-ignoring planner's Bayesian regret is therefore exactly **T·Δ_OL**: linear, with an explicit constant. In the squared-error special case, the constant is the prior predictive variance x ᵀΣ₀x.

The zero-advantage condition is equally sharp, and it is the "exactly when" half of the headline. If the disturbances ε_t are fresh i.i.d. draws from a *known* law and the current state is observed, the controlled process is an MDP in s_t. Markov policies are then optimal, and past transitions are worth exactly nothing beyond the current state. Advantage requires a persistent unknown. That unknown can hide inside apparently i.i.d. noise: if the noise *distribution* is unknown, the disturbances are exchangeable but not independent (de Finetti), and that dependence is precisely what hindsight exploits. Even with a persistent unknown, the advantage is zero when one action is optimal for all θ in the prior's support, or when the observations are uninformative about the decision-relevant functional of θ. Formally this is the EVSI = 0 condition: the value is affine over the reachable posteriors. No single primary source states this lemma in hindsight-planning language. It follows from Markov optimality and should be presented as a lemma with its short proof rather than cited as a named theorem.

Two caveats bound every claim built on this foundation. First, the guarantee concerns the *Bayes optimum over the larger class*, not any particular approximate adaptive planner. A planner that infers θ under a misspecified model can do worse than open-loop. Grünwald and van Ommen show posteriors concentrating on increasingly bad models as data grows under heteroskedastic misspecification ([arXiv:1412.3730](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/1412.3730)). Esponda and Pouzo's Berk–Nash equilibrium shows misspecified learners settling permanently on action-dependent KL-best-fit beliefs and suboptimal actions ([Econometrica](https://www.econometricsociety.org/doi/10.3982/ECTA12609)). This is the theoretical home of "residual reuse hurts when there is no disturbance": transporting pure model error is acting on a misspecified posterior. Second, information can have negative value in games ([Bassan et al. 2003](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:spr:jogath:v:32:y:2003:i:1:p:17-31)), which matters only for multi-agent settings.

The Bayes-adaptive MDP is the exact object a hindsight planner approximates. Its hyperstate is (s, posterior over θ), and its optimum automatically includes the dual-control value of informative actions ([Duff 2002](https://scholarworks.umass.edu/dissertations/AAI3039353)). BAMCP approximates it by Monte-Carlo tree search with root sampling, and its authors state that it converges to the Bayes-optimal policy as simulations grow ([Guez, Silver & Dayan 2012](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2012/file/35051070e572e47d2c26c241ab88307f-Paper.pdf)). The exact theorem statement was not checked. The most useful structural distinction for a paper is **passive versus active learning**. If the observed transitions are equally informative about θ whatever action is taken, the posterior evolves exogenously, and the certainty-equivalent policy for the current posterior captures the benefit without exploration. If informativeness depends on the action, a greedy "condition on hindsight but never probe" planner can lock in on a wrong action.

## Identifiability through the action splits log T from √T

Once the advantage is known to be T·EVPI minus a learning cost, the question becomes how large that learning cost is. The answer depends almost entirely on whether the hidden quantity is identified by free exogenous excitation or only through the planner's own actions.

### Passive identification costs (d/2) log T, paid once

In the Gaussian linear model y_t = x_tᵀθ + ε_t with θ ~ N(0, Σ₀) and adaptively chosen x_t, the posterior-mean predictor's per-step excess squared error is x_tᵀΣ_{t−1}x_t. The chain rule for Gaussian mutual information gives **I(θ; y_{1:T}) = ½ Σ_t log(1 + x_tᵀΣ_{t−1}x_t/σ²) = ½ log det(I + σ⁻²Σ₀^{1/2}XᵀXΣ₀^{1/2}) ≤ (d/2) log(1 + TλL²/(dσ²))**. Linearizing the log then bounds cumulative excess squared error by O(d log T). This is the same identity that drives GP-UCB's information-gain analysis ([Srinivas et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/0912.3995)). Clarke and Barron show this is generic rather than Gaussian-specific: the cumulative Bayes log-loss redundancy equals I(θ; data) and grows as **(d/2) log(n/2πe) + ½ log det I(θ) + log(1/w(θ)) + o(1)** for smooth d-dimensional families ([Clarke & Barron 1990](https://researchr.org/publication/ClarkeB90/bibtex); restated in [arXiv:quant-ph/9612043](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/9612043)). The frequentist counterpart is the elliptical potential lemma and the self-normalized martingale bound of Abbasi-Yadkori, Pál and Szepesvári. Their Theorem 2 gives the ridge confidence ellipsoid with radius R√(d log((1+tL²/λ)/δ)) + λ^{1/2}S ([NeurIPS 2011](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2011/file/e1d5be1c7f2f456670de3d53c7b54f4a-Paper.pdf)).

The consequence for planning is direct. If planning loss is locally quadratic in model error, cumulative hindsight regret is O(d log T), against Θ(T) for the outcome-ignoring planner. If the loss is only Lipschitz, Cauchy–Schwarz gives O(√(Td log T)). **Mania, Tu and Recht** supply exactly the needed quadratic link for control: the certainty-equivalent controller's suboptimality "scales as the square of the parameter error" for LQR and partially observed LQG ([arXiv:1902.07826](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.07826)). Lai and Wei give the consistency condition for least squares with adaptively chosen regressors. If the information matrix grows in every direction with λ_min outgrowing log λ_max, the error is about log λ_max/λ_min, which is Õ(1/t) under persistent excitation ([Annals of Statistics 1982](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/annals-of-statistics/volume-10/issue-1/Least-Squares-Estimates-in-Stochastic-Regression-Models-with-Applications-to/10.1214/aos/1176345697.full)). The exact o-vs-O form and moment condition were not verified against the full text.

### Probing costs √T, and the LQR literature proves the boundary

Online LQR is the cleanest place where the dividing line is a theorem. With both A and B unknown, **Simchowitz and Foster prove the optimal regret is Θ̃(√(d_u²d_xT))** and rule out any poly(log T) algorithm. A simple certainty-equivalent controller with exploratory noise attains the bound ([arXiv:2001.09576](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.09576)). The mechanism is collinearity. Under u = K̂x, every regressor (x, u) lies in a d_x-dimensional subspace, so the d_u orthogonal directions receive no information unless noise of variance σ² is injected at cost σ²T. Balancing that against ~log T/σ² of quadratic estimation loss gives σ² ~ T^{−1/2} and √T regret. **Cassel, Cohen and Koren** prove the complementary positive result: polylogarithmic regret when only A is unknown, or only B with a non-degenerate optimal policy, together with a lower bound that "square root regret is unavoidable" when the non-degeneracy fails ([arXiv:2002.08095](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.08095)). My belief that the condition is full row rank of K* is unverified. In the partially observed setting, Lale et al. obtain polylog(T) regret for unknown LQG via closed-loop identification ([arXiv:2003.11227](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.11227)). That result depends on their strong-convexity and excitation assumptions, and I did not verify the exact exponent. Earlier OFU work established Õ(√T) for the general case ([Abbasi-Yadkori & Szepesvári 2011](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v19/abbasi-yadkori11a.html)).

The generic bandit lower bounds frame these rates. Lai and Robbins give Ω(log T) instance-dependent regret for any consistent policy ([reference](https://cs.utexas.edu/~shivaram/readings/b2hd-LaiRobbins1985.html)). Lattimore and Szepesvári show that minimax regret equals worst-case Bayesian regret ([arXiv:1902.00470](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1902.00470)), so √T minimax lower bounds also bound Bayesian regret under a least-favourable prior. Russo and Van Roy's information ratio gives the active-learning upper bound: **Bayesian regret ≤ √(Γ̄·H(A*)·T)**, with Γ̄ ≤ K/2 for K arms, d/2 for linear bandits and 1/2 under full information ([JMLR 2016](https://jmlr.org/papers/v17/14-087.html)). That last value is the bandit-theoretic signature of passive learning.

Mapping these onto disturbance types gives the most theorem-ready taxonomy for a hindsight-planning paper. An **additive hidden disturbance** (y = f(x,u) + d) is read off residuals whatever action is taken. It is the analogue of "A unknown", so hindsight re-planning costs O(log T) while forward planning costs Θ(‖d‖²T). A **multiplicative gain** on the action (y = f(x) + b·u) is identified only in proportion to u. It is the analogue of "B unknown": log T if planned actions persistently excite the affected directions, Θ̃(√T) with forced exploration otherwise, and possibly outright non-convergence without exploration. That last case is the closed-loop identifiability trap OpTTA invokes from Borkar and Varaiya ([arXiv:2610.35636](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35636)). The fault-diagnosis literature draws the same additive-versus-multiplicative line. It notes that multiplicative faults are "a more challenging task due to the nonlinear impact of the fault" ([arXiv:2011.13730](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.13730); [Blanke et al.](https://www.springer.com/gp/book/9783662479421)). The additive-to-multiplicative mapping onto Cassel's regimes is my inference; no source gives regret rates for multiplicative-fault estimation as such.

## Kalman gain and change-point theory say how much hindsight to keep

Offset-free MPC is the oldest exact theorem of "hindsight beats forward planning", and the one a world-model paper should explicitly generalize. The plant model is augmented with integrating disturbance states, x⁺ = Ax + Bu + B_d d, d⁺ = d, y = Cx + C_d d. These states are estimated with an observer or Kalman filter, and the MPC target is shifted by the estimate. If the augmented system is detectable and the number of disturbance states equals the number of tracked outputs, the controlled outputs track with **zero steady-state offset** despite constant unmeasured disturbances and plant–model mismatch ([Pannocchia & Rawlings 2003](https://arpi.unipi.it/handle/11568/77644); [Maeder, Borrelli & Morari 2009](https://portal.mardi4nfdi.de/wiki/Publication:1049078)). A nominal MPC without the estimate generically keeps a non-zero offset, so its cost grows linearly in T. The detectability rank condition, rank [[A − I, B_d],[C, C_d]] = n + n_d, is the control-theoretic identifiability condition. A disturbance direction invisible in the outputs cannot be inferred from residuals, and hindsight gives nothing there. The rank form and the n_d = p requirement come from domain knowledge and should be checked against the papers' lemma numbering. The counterfactual-world-model paper itself places disturbance observers, offset-free MPC and iterative learning control among the counterfactual estimators "usually analyzed with a known nominal model" ([arXiv:2611.24778](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24778)). That sentence is the bridge a latent-world-model paper should cross.

The Kalman filter makes "how much of the last residual to reuse" a closed-form quantity. In the local level model y_t = μ_t + ε_t, μ_{t+1} = μ_t + η_t, with signal-to-noise ratio q = σ_η²/σ_ε², the steady-state gain is **K̄ = (√(q² + 4q) − q)/2**. It rises monotonically from 0 to 1 ([Aarhus state-space notes](https://econ.au.dk/fileadmin/user_upload/2016Aarhus_Day1.pdf); [Antwerp notes](https://medialibrary.uantwerpen.be/files/7228/c5185dc4-4483-43f4-924f-5f9ca0978070.pdf)). The research notes derived this independently, with K̄ = 1 − 1/q + O(q⁻²) as q → ∞ and K̄ ≈ √q as q → 0. Residual reuse (forecast = last observed outcome, d̂_{t+1} = d̂_t + 1·r_t) is therefore precisely the **q → ∞ limit**. It is optimal only when disturbance innovation swamps measurement and model noise. For a truly constant disturbance (q = 0), the gain decays as 1/t and the filter becomes the running mean, i.e. recursive least squares, recovering the Lai–Wei 1/t error rate.

### A derived persistence threshold that matches the empirical pattern

A short calculation of my own (not taken from a source) turns this into the "exactly when" criterion that the latent-world-model experiments call for. Take a scalar disturbance d_t with stationary variance σ_d², AR(1) persistence φ, and measurement or model noise σ_ε². The outcome-blind forecast (prior mean) has error variance σ_d² + σ_ε². Full residual reuse (forecast y_t) has error variance 2σ_d²(1 − φ) + 2σ_ε². **Residual reuse beats outcome-blind planning iff φ > ½(1 + σ_ε²/σ_d²)**. A fresh disturbance (φ = 0) exactly doubles the error. A constant disturbance (φ = 1) wins iff σ_d² > σ_ε². With no disturbance at all, reuse doubles the noise-driven error, because it transports pure model error. The Kalman/EWMA gain interpolates optimally between the two, and with q → 0 the ratio of K = 1 forecast variance to the optimal one tends to 2. This toy matches the anchor paper's measurements qualitatively. Under a persistent fault, residual transport improved three-step success from 4–25% to 11–51%. Fresh disturbances changed success by −4 to +6 points. With no disturbance, correction cost 4–15 points. Command-specific analysis showed the residual removing 83% of error for a same-event retry, 75% for the next transition under a persistent fault, and −137% under a fresh disturbance ([arXiv:2611.24778](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24778)). The paper reports this pattern but proves nothing about it. Lifting the scalar threshold to vector latent residuals with an interaction term is a concrete, provable contribution.

### Switching disturbances demand calibrated forgetting

When the hidden disturbance changes, the theory of piecewise-stationary bandits gives exact prices for both remembering too much and too little. **Garivier and Moulines** prove that sliding-window UCB with window τ ≈ 2B√(T log T/Υ_T) attains O(√(Υ_T T log T)) with Υ_T breakpoints. They also prove that any policy with logarithmic stationary regret "cannot reach a regret of order smaller than T/log T in presence of breakpoints" ([arXiv:0805.3415](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/0805.3415)). Infinite memory, which is what a hindsight planner that accumulates all past residuals amounts to, is near-linear. Zero memory is linear. The minimax rate with N segments and K arms is Ω(√(NKT)) ([Zhou et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.10402)). Adaptive procedures reach √(LT) without knowing L ([Auer, Gajane & Ortner, COLT 2019](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v99/auer19a.html); refined to significant switches by [Suk & Kpotufe](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.13838)). Detect-and-restart schemes reach O(√(T A Υ_T log T)) on instances where changes are large enough to detect ([Besson et al., GLR-klUCB](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.01575)). Under a drift budget instead of switches, the rate becomes T^{2/3}: (K log K)^{1/3}V_T^{1/3}T^{2/3} for bandits with a matching lower bound ([Besbes, Gur & Zeevi](https://arxiv.org/abs/1405.3316)). The linear-bandit analogue needs care. Zhao et al. found a flaw that also affects the sliding-window and weighted analyses, and corrected rates are weaker ([arXiv:2103.05324](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.05324v2)). For RL, the black-box **MASTER** reduction converts any optimal stationary learner into one with Õ(min{√(LT), Δ^{1/3}T^{2/3}}) dynamic regret without prior knowledge, across bandits and episodic and infinite-horizon MDPs ([Wei & Luo 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.05406)). Model-free RestartQ-UCB is optimal in Δ^{1/3}T^{2/3} up to an H^{1/3} factor ([Mao et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.03161)). Sliding-window UCRL needs confidence widening to keep the optimistic MDP's diameter bounded ([Cheung, Simchi-Levi & Zhu](https://arxiv.org/html/1906.02922v4)).

Quickest change detection supplies the delay side of the trade-off. CUSUM is exactly optimal for Lorden's worst-case delay criterion under a false-alarm constraint E_∞[τ] ≥ γ ([Moustakides 1986](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/annals-of-statistics/volume-14/issue-4/Optimal-Stopping-Times-for-Detecting-Changes-in-Distributions/10.1214/aos/1176350164.full)), with delay ≈ log γ/KL(post‖pre) ([Lorden 1971](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/annals-of-mathematical-statistics/volume-42/issue-6/Procedures-for-Reacting-to-a-Change-in-Distribution/10.1214/aoms/1177693055.full)). For a hindsight planner this means each switch costs roughly delay × per-step gap, and each false alarm costs a re-learning burn-in. Setting γ polynomial in T keeps false alarms negligible. Bayesian online change-point detection gives the principled "surprise-triggered reset" as a run-length posterior ([Adams & MacKay](https://arxiv.org/abs/0710.3742)). But its use inside a planner has only empirical support ([Mellor & Shapiro](https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.3721)). **No regret bound exists for BOCPD-driven resets in a planning loop, and none for change detection on learned world-model residuals.**

## Latent world models have prediction-level theorems but no planning theorems

### The Counterfactual World Models paper defines the identification frontier

The paper by Balestriero, LeCun and Varaku (arXiv 2611.24778, 18 November 2026) is the anchor, and its results should be cited at full precision ([arXiv:2611.24778](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24778)). Its negative results establish that hindsight planning *requires* a structural assumption. Proposition 2.1 exhibits two structural models that agree on every action-conditional outcome law but disagree on the alternative outcome for the same observed transition. Theorem 2.2 (factual gauge nonidentification) shows that for any family of ν-preserving bijections G_{s,a}, the mechanisms T∘G induce identical factual laws but different counterfactual couplings unless the gauge is action-independent. So likelihood, calibration or any criterion depending only on factual laws cannot identify the same-disturbance counterfactual. The converse holds too: every factual-law-preserving reparameterization has this form. Theorem H.2 makes this a sharp minimax impossibility. With U ~ Unif(0,1) and the two worlds (U,U) and (U,1−U), even infinite unpaired data leaves pointwise risk (y − ½)², 1/12 on average and ¼ in the worst case. This is the world-model instance of the causal hierarchy theorem: lower-layer data generically underdetermine counterfactuals ([Bareinboim et al.](https://causalai.net/r60.pdf)). Neural causal models inherit it regardless of expressivity ([Xia et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.00793)), and MDP transitions alone do not identify counterfactuals ([Oberst & Sontag](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.05824)).

The positive results define the regime where hindsight planning can be proved better. Definition 3.1 sets **ẑ_{t+1}(a′) = z_{t+1} + μ(H_t,a′) − μ(H_t,a_t)**, with a readout variant that differs when the readout is nonlinear. The exact error identity says the mean squared error equals tr Var_ν(Δ), the variance across disturbances of the individual action contrast Δ = Y(a′) − Y(a). This is the gap between individual and conditional average treatment effects, so residual reuse is exact iff the contrast is disturbance-independent. Proposition 3.2 proves **uniqueness**: any rule exact in every additive world F = m(z,a) + q(z,ε) must be residual reuse, and the counterfactual is identified even though ε itself is not. Theorem 3.3 bounds the error by the mixed derivative: E‖T_R − Y(a′)‖² ≤ K_z²‖a′ − a‖² tr Cov(ε). This gives relative risk **ρ_z = tr Var(Δ)/tr Var(Y(a′)) ≤ (K_z‖a′−a‖/κ_z)²**, and ρ < 1 iff residual reuse beats the best outcome-blind predictor. Theorem 3.4 shows that with separate smooth action and disturbance channels the interaction is second order in duration h, so ρ_h = O(h²). Theorem 3.6 proves this is **minimax rate-optimal with a sharp leading constant**: 4C²h⁴/(1+C²h²) ≤ inf sup risk ≤ 4C²h⁴, even for an estimator that knows exact factual laws. The paper notes explicitly that repeated observations of a shared disturbance may permit better estimators, which is an open lever.

The stated failure modes coincide with the √T regime of adaptive control. Multiplicative (gain) faults interact at first order. On an offset-to-gain interpolation, theory predicts ρ = 0, 0.5 and 2, and the paper measures 0.50/0.48 and 2.01/1.97. Contacts and saturation (Push-T, ρ > 1) and folded coordinates (MAE-B raw features on the boat task, ρ = 1.92) also break the rule. The interaction ratio "needs paired outcomes" and cannot be certified from factual logs, although factual data can flag a large one. In one-transition hindsight planning (CEM with 256 candidates, success at standardized distance < 0.2), the counterfactual world model reached **37/60/69/50/62%** success on HandReach/Ball-in-Cup/FetchReach/BlueROV2/Liquid transport. A true-simulator oracle that does not see the outcome reached **15/15/14/23/19%**, and the trained forward predictor 10–18%. The gain is positive with bootstrap confidence intervals above zero in all ten environment–encoder cells. Two same-event retries raise success to 51–97%. The authors caution that the outcome-blind oracle minimizes squared distance, so it "is a reference for that objective, not a universal upper bound on thresholded success". The planning comparison is empirical, single-transition, at most three steps, and simulated.

| [AdaJEPA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.32026) | Slowly varying model error | One gradient step per chunk on last layers, 5-transition buffer, reset each episode | PointMaze 53.3→78.7%; small gains on mass/damping shifts | None |
| [JEPA-TTT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00722) | Persistent shift across episodes | Predictor-only self-supervised updates, persistent buffer | Latent MSE −83%, best score 0.267→0.678 | None |
| [sft-JEPA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29453) | Offset plus action-dependent error | Fast (reset) + slow (LoRA) learners | Offset is 43–79% of error; offset alone recovers 91% of AdaJEPA gain | Empirical decomposition |
| [OpTTA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35636) | Planning-relevant model error | Prediction loss + RBMLE-style planning loss | +13.5 vs +6.9 points over frozen; wins 14/15 | Cites RBMLE; no proof |
| [Look Back (RLB)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29259) | Event-level noise (SCM) | Counterfactual WM as RLOO baseline | Collisions 7.89→3.77% | Unbiasedness, Hellinger sample complexity |
| [Dynamics-randomization headroom](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32414) | Context θ | Defines I(θ; s′ \| s,a) | Visitation removes 88% of Wind headroom | Definition, no bounds |

Three of these results bear directly on a theory of hindsight planning. **sft-JEPA's finding** that an action-independent offset "effectively shifts the planner's goal", ẑ(a) − g = z⁺(a) + d(a) − (g − b), is the same object as residual reuse viewed through gradient descent. An offset cancels in action contrasts but not in distance-to-goal costs. Its observation that reusing the previous episode's shift costs 26 points (16.0% vs 42.7%) is the persistence threshold seen empirically ([arXiv:2611.29453](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29453)). **OpTTA's** diagnosis is that prediction-only adaptation fits only the agent's own actions and "can remain wrong about the actions that would reach the goal". This is the Borkar–Varaiya closed-loop identifiability failure in latent form, and the overlap condition any regret bound must contain ([arXiv:2610.35636](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35636)). The **predictive headroom** paper defines I_emp = I_d(θ; s′ | s,a) as the minimum one-step log-loss gain from revealing context. It shows that state often already encodes the context, so visited-state headroom is 0.077 vs 0.871 under the prior on MiniGrid Wind, and resets raise it by up to 833% ([arXiv:2610.32414](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32414)). This is a candidate "value of hindsight information", but it is defined only for one-step prediction, not planning.

Look Back is the only 2026 world-model paper with substantive propositions ([arXiv:2611.29259](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29259)). Maximum-likelihood counterfactual models over a finite class have squared Hellinger error ≤ (1 + log|Ω|)/N. Counterfactual models are provably easier to learn than world models when environmental variation dominates action-induced change (E² > 48A²(3i+4)(1+log|Ω|)). Most importantly, counterfactual baselines from *any* SCM consistent with the MDP give unbiased policy gradients (Corollary 1), so **credit assignment needs no identification, but hindsight planning does**. A retry or diagnosis must hit the true coupling, which is why additivity or another structural restriction is unavoidable in the planning theorem. Identification can also be restored by bijective generation mechanisms with known structure ([Nasr-Esfahany et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.02228)), though multi-dimensional bijective mechanisms are generally not identified ([arXiv:2301.09031](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.09031)). Scalar monotone responses are identified by rank transport, and paired or simulator data identify counterfactuals outright ([arXiv:2611.24778](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24778)).

### Meta-RL context inference is Bayes-adaptive only in the limit

PEARL samples a task latent from a permutation-invariant posterior and holds it per episode ([arXiv:1903.08254](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.08254)). VariBAD conditions the policy on a variational belief to approximate Bayes-adaptive behaviour ([arXiv:1910.08348](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.08348)). RMA regresses predefined privileged extrinsics from recent history ([arXiv:2107.04034](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.04034)). All are empirical. Memory-based meta-learners approximate Bayes-optimal agents in principle ([Ortega et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.03030)), and the regret templates come from tabular settings: PSRL's Õ(τS√(AT)) Bayesian regret ([Osband et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1306.0940)) and a 2026 information-directed contextual-POMDP bound scaling with log|C| ([arXiv:2602.03939](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03939), not verified in depth). The Exo-MDP hindsight-learning line gives the essential caveat. Hindsight-optimal values computed on realized exogenous traces are biased "towards foresight" ([Sinclair et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.06272); [Look Back](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29259)). Any theorem must specify *which* objective improves: counterfactual decision accuracy for the same event (retry, diagnosis) or forward performance while a disturbance persists. These are different claims with different oracles.

### Hierarchy under disturbances has no regret theory at all

Hierarchical world-model planners are empirical, and their "hindsight" means something else. Director re-plans latent goals every 8 steps inside DreamerV2 ([arXiv:2206.04114](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.04114)). LeCun's H-JEPA is a position paper ([OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=BZ5a1r-kVsf)). HIRO's hindsight relabelling corrects non-stationarity of the *lower-level policy during training*, not exogenous disturbances ([arXiv:1805.08296](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.08296)). Options can provably reduce regret, but only in stationary MDPs ([Fruit & Lazaric](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.08667)). Provable hierarchy-based meta-RL adapts across tasks, not within-task switches ([Chua, Lei & Lee 2023](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v206/chua23a.html)). Hierarchical tube MPC guarantees recursive feasibility and constraint satisfaction under bounded disturbances, not regret ([Kögel et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.14269)). No theorem shows that hierarchy plus change-point adaptation beats flat adaptive or hierarchical non-adaptive planning. A natural conjecture by analogy with per-arm restarts in GLR-klUCB is that switches confined to low-level dynamics need only low-level restarts, replacing √(LT·total complexity) with √(LT·low-level complexity). It is unproven.

## The provable theorem is assembled, but not yet proved, for JEPA models

The pieces most relevant to "hindsight planning is provably and substantially better than forward planning, and exactly when" rank as follows. First, the **EVPI-linear lower bound for outcome-ignoring planners** together with the **zero-advantage lemma** (no persistent unknown, or a θ-robust action) give both the size and the necessary condition. Second, the **Clarke–Barron / log-det information identity** combined with **Mania et al.'s quadratic suboptimality** gives O(d log T) hindsight regret in the passive regime. Third, the **Simchowitz–Foster and Cassel et al. dichotomy** proves where log T becomes √T, mapping onto additive vs gain faults. Fourth, the **Counterfactual World Models theorems** give the identification frontier for one transition in latent space: nonidentification, uniqueness, the interaction bound and O(h²) minimax optimality. Fifth, **offset-free MPC and the Kalman gain formula** give the control-theoretic template and the persistence-dependent optimal reuse weight. Sixth, **Garivier–Moulines' T/log T result and MASTER's √(LT)** price forgetting under switches.

What is missing is the composition, and each missing piece is an explicitly acknowledged or evidenced gap. The most valuable result would be a **decision-level theorem**: for an L-Lipschitz (or locally quadratic) goal cost in readout coordinates, the hindsight planner's suboptimality against the true-disturbance oracle is bounded by roughly L·√(tr Var Δ) plus action-contrast learning error plus search error, against an outcome-blind floor of roughly L·√(tr Var Y(a′)). That would yield "hindsight beats forward iff ρ < τ(L, tolerance, ε_μ)" in place of the prediction-level ρ < 1. A **thresholded-success version**, via anti-concentration of Y(a′) and concentration of Δ, would explain the measured 14–23% vs 35–69% gap that the squared-loss oracle does not. A **persistence theorem** would lift the scalar AR(1) threshold above to vector latent residuals with interaction, giving the optimal shrinkage (the Kalman gain) and explaining why correction hurts under fresh or absent disturbances. That would unify residual reuse (zero-step, closed-form) with AdaJEPA/sft-JEPA gradient TTA (smoothed, persistent offset estimates) as points on one filter-gain axis, with OpTTA's coverage term added. **Multi-step chaining** bounds, under contraction of ∂F/∂s and bounded interaction, would show when reused residuals compound linearly rather than exponentially, against compounding outcome-blind rollouts. **Repeated-observation rates** with k retries of a shared disturbance should beat the single-outcome O(h²) minimax bound, as the authors conjecture and the 51–97% retry results suggest. **Gain and contact transport rules** (command-rescaled or rank transport for monotone scalar gains, mode-conditional reuse for contacts) need matching lower bounds showing plain reuse is first-order suboptimal (ρ → 2), and the LQR theory predicts a √T exploration price for gains. **Switching disturbances** need detection-delay vs regret trade-offs on latent residuals, which neither BOCPD nor any world-model paper supplies. Finally, a **learned-additivity regularizer** for JEPA encoders, bounding ρ in latent space by penalizing the action–residual interaction, would address the ρ = 1.92 failure of raw features. It would pair naturally with factually checkable one-sided tests or a minimal paired-trial budget that certifies ρ < τ. None of T2-Z, AdaJEPA, JEPA-TTT, sft-JEPA or OpTTA proves convergence, regret or latent identifiability, so even modest guarantees for those methods would be new.

## Conclusion

The question "is hindsight planning better?" has a trivial answer ("weakly, always, at the optimum"). The question "by how much?" has an answer in rates rather than constants, and the rate is set by identifiability through the action. That reframes the latent-world-model results. Residual reuse's O(h²) exactness for additive, separate-channel physics and its first-order failure for gain faults are the latent-space shadow of the log T vs √T split proved for LQR. The empirical persistence pattern (helps under persistent faults, neutral under fresh noise, harmful with no disturbance) is what a Kalman gain set to 1 should produce. The strongest paper available today would therefore not invent new machinery. It would prove that a JEPA hindsight planner inherits the classical Θ(T) − O(d log T) separation when the latent disturbance is additive and persistent, that the advantage vanishes at an explicit persistence threshold, and that gain faults, contacts and switches move it into √T or detection-limited regimes.

The sharpest open risk is the one Blackwell does not cover. Every guarantee is about a correctly specified Bayes optimum, while practical hindsight planners transport residuals through learned, possibly misspecified encoders whose interaction ratio cannot be certified from factual data. A theorem that makes ρ (or an information analogue such as I(ε; Y(a′) | s, a, Y(a))) both learnable and decision-relevant would turn "residual reuse works in our simulators" into "hindsight planning is provably better, here is the boundary". The literature read for this report contains no such theorem as of late November 2026, and that absence is based on the papers and related-work sections reviewed here, not an exhaustive search.
